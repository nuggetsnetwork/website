Tunis, Tunisia

Built: 2026-11-01. Range: 550 BCE to 1881 (rail 590 BCE to 1920). Opens at: 1420. Chapter 37. Data: src/data/tunis.ts, src/data/tunis-realms.ts. Tunis is the anchor chapter for Tunisia.

The scope choice

Tunis is a small town beside the ruins of Carthage, 15 km away on the same lagoon coast. This chapter covers both under one name: the houses that held Carthage (the Carthaginian regime, Rome, the Vandals, the Byzantine exarchate) come first, and after the Arab capture of 698 the houses that held Tunis. The plan of the city runs from Bardo to Carthage and La Goulette, about 20 km wide, and the character line of each early house starts "Carthage, then Tunis". A shorter chapter, Tunis proper, would start in 698 and drop four houses and five monuments.

Exclusions

  • The legendary foundation by Dido and the traditional date of 814 BCE: no page gives a firm date, and legend stays out; the range starts with Mago I about 550 BCE. A longer chapter would add the first three centuries of Carthage.
  • The Magonids Hannibal I (410 to 406 BCE), Mago II (396 to 375 BCE), Hanno III and Himilco I, and the Great Hannos: curated for the court's readability, or no usable date.
  • Carthaginian leaders between 340 and 247 BCE (Bomilcar, Hanno the Great, Hasdrubal's successors) and the general Mago Barca, Hasdrubal Barca: no monuments and curation.
  • A century of waste: Rome's province of Africa from 146 BCE had Utica as its capital and the site of Carthage was left waste apart from Gracchus's colony of 122 BCE; the rail shows a real gap of about a century, 145 to 45 BCE.
  • The Roman Republic and Roman emperors other than those in the data; Gordian I's brief 238 reign (no page read ties him to Carthage) and Theodosius I.
  • The Vandal period's monuments and Byzantine monuments: no page read gives a surviving building with a date and coordinates, so both houses have none.
  • The Byzantine prefects Solomon, Germanus and John Troglita: no page read names them.
  • Governors of Ifriqiya between 698 and 800 other than the four listed, and Abd al-Rahman ibn Habib's independent rule.
  • The Spanish interlude of 1535 to 1573: folded into the Hafsid house, whose sultans were Spanish tributaries and who reigned in Tunis; it is longer than the usual fifteen-year fold, and a separate house would overlap the Hafsids. The Ottomans' brief hold of 1534 to 1535 and 1569 to 1573 is folded too.
  • Hafsid sultans of 1277 to 1318, 1346 to 1350, 1369 to 1370 and 1434 to 1435, and four of the sixteenth-century sultans before Moulay Hasan: curated.
  • Ottoman pashas of 1574 to 1591 (no page names them), the later Muradid beys and Murad III.
  • Husainid beys Uthman (reigned two months in 1814) and every bey after 1881.
  • Monuments left out: Dar Hussein (no builder, date or coordinates on its page), Bab el Bhar (its page gives no date and says it has been altered many times), the Sidi Bu Khrisan mausoleum (1093; no coordinates), El Jedid Mosque (1723 to 1727) and Saheb Ettabaa Mosque (1808 to 1814) (no coordinates), Chikly fort (1546 to 1550), Borj Ali Raïs (page not found), Sidi Bou Said (no page read), Kairouan's Great Mosque (not in the Tunis area), the Cathedral of Saint Louis (1884, colonial), the Roman theatre, odeon and circus of Carthage, the cisterns of La Malga and the Zaghouan aqueduct.
  • The French protectorate from 1881 and independence from 1956: colonial; the Husainid beys remained on the throne until 1957 but the executive passed to the French Resident-General. A longer chapter would run to the abolition of the monarchy on 25 July 1957.

Judgement calls

  • The chapter ends on the Treaty of Bardo of 1881, not in 1957, following the rule used for Fez in 1912: the range stops at the last state-level sovereign and colonial administration is excluded. Flag: a longer chapter would run to 1957; the Husainid house remained on the throne for 76 more years.
  • The range starts in about 550 BCE with Mago I, not at the traditional date of 814 BCE, because the legend has no firm date on the page read.
  • The Carthage houses are told as "Carthage, then Tunis" and the plan frames both. The Tunis houses of the first three centuries after 698 are small: Tunis was a garrison town and the capital was Kairouan, so the caliphal house has only four rulers and no monument and the Fatimid and Zirid house has no monument.
  • The Carthaginian regime is one house, because it was a republic whose leaders were elected suffetes and generals, treated as a regime in the way the Athenian democracy is; its Magonids and Barcids share it. The rulers listed leave gaps (after Mago III in 344 BCE and before Hamilcar Barca in 247 BCE), because the men who led Carthage in between are left out for curation; the house itself runs on unbroken.
  • Roman Carthage starts in -44, not -146, because the city did not exist from 145 to 45 BCE; Julius Caesar's reign is the single year 44 BCE. Augustus is linked to him as "nephew" although he was great-nephew and adopted son.
  • The Fatimids and the Zirids are one house (910 to 1058) because the Zirids governed as the Fatimids' viceroys until the break of 1041 to 1051. Al-Mu'izz ibn Badis's reign is set to end in 1058 although he reigned to 1062 elsewhere; the Banu Khurasan start in 1059 on the Banu Khurasan page.
  • Hand-off years: the Vandals' Gelimer ends in 533, a year before the surrender of 534; the Byzantine house starts in 534; the Arab capture of 698 starts the caliphal house the following year after the last Byzantine exarch (697); Sinan Pasha's reign is 1575, although the conquest was in 1574, so that the Ottoman house starts the year after the Hafsids end.
  • The Banu Khurasan's Hammadid interlude of 1128 to 1148 and Ali ibn Ahmad's five months of 1159 are folded into the house; the line has a gap in rulers from 1129 to 1147.
  • Monuments placed in a ruler's reign that did not build them, or whose date is a guess: the Tophet and the Byrsa under Mago I and Hasdrubal the Boetharch (the Tophet is of the ninth century BCE or about 650 BCE, the Byrsa's date is 146 BCE, its fall); the Zitouna under Muhammad II at 864 (founded about 698, in the form it has from the Aghlabid rebuilding); the Bardo under Abu Faris at 1420, a placement; the Youssef Dey Mosque at 1631 (its infobox gives 1612, and the text says 1631); the amphitheatre under Hadrian at 135 (a placement within the inscription's 133 to 139); the baths at 162 (the year the page's range ends, a year after Antoninus's death).
  • Where the Zaytuna Mosque belongs: the 698 foundation and 991 dome belong to earlier houses, but the mosque stands once, in the house that gave it its form.
  • Reign starts shifted to keep hand-offs and parents earlier than children: Hasdrubal I's end of -511, Hanno the Navigator's start of -479, Huneric's start of 478, Gunthamund's of 485, Thrasamund's of 497 and similar one-year shifts. The Husainid bey Ali I starts in 1736 although the page gives 1735, so that he starts after Husayn I.
