Angkor, Cambodia

Built: 2026-11-01. Range: 802 to 1431 (rail 770 to 1470). Opens at: 1190. Chapter 35. Data: src/data/angkor.ts, src/data/angkor-realms.ts.

Sources

All read on 2026-11-01 through the page-fetch tool, which summarises a page rather than printing it. Pages longer than 100,000 characters were cut at that point. Angkor is the anchor chapter for Cambodia. The Khmer kings' dates come from inscriptions and from Chinese notices, and the Wikipedia pages disagree with each other by years, so every reign before about 1300 is approximate.

  • https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khmer_Empire (characters 0 to 100,000 and 100,000 to 200,000 of 226,700; the last 26,700 were not read): the table of rulers from Jayavarman II (802 to 835) to Barom Reachea II (1405 to 1431), the capitals, Champa wars, Jayavarman VII's reign and buildings, Zhou Daguan, the decline and the 1431 abandonment. The page does not say which chronology it follows; this chapter follows its table, and it notes that the table, the infobox and the style tables give different years (see Judgement calls).
  • https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angkor (characters 0 to 100,000 of 143,426): the successive capitals, the dates and builders of the main monuments, the post-1431 history (Angkor Wat stayed a Buddhist shrine, a 1586 Portuguese visitor, Japanese inscriptions of the seventeenth century, Mouhot in 1860). It says Angkor was sacked by Ayutthaya in 1431 and the page for Ponhea Yat gives a different account (below).
  • https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jayavarman_II: his dates (about 770 to 850 in the lead, a reign of 802 to 835 in the table), the dispute over what 'Java' means, the 802 ceremony on Mount Kulen, that the Sdok Kak Thom inscription of 1052 is the main source, and that no inscription of his own survives.
  • https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suryavarman_II: his accession in 1113 by force against a great-uncle, Dharanindravarman I, the 1145 sack of Vijaya, the failed attacks on Dai Viet, Angkor Wat, and his death about 1150 (the page gives 1145 or 1150).
  • https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jayavarman_VII: the 1177 Cham sack and the four-year occupation, his coronation in 1181, the conquest of Champa in 1190 and 1191, the reach to Vientiane and the Kra Isthmus, his buildings, his Buddhism, his death about 1218.
  • https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahidharapura_dynasty: the dynasty's twelve kings (Hiranyavarman to Jayavarman IX), 1080 to 1336, that Dharanindravarman II was a descendant of Jayavarman VI and father of Jayavarman VII, and that Suryavarman I is not in it.
  • https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suryavarman_I: the reign (1002 in the infobox, 1006 in the navbox), his defeat of Udayadityavarman I about 1002, the war with Jayaviravarman to 1010, the Chola contact, his reach to Lopburi, Preah Vihear, Phimeanakas and Ta Keo.
  • https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yasovarman_I: 889 to 910, son of Indravarman I, the succession war, Yasodharapura, Phnom Bakheng, the East Baray, Lolei and his sons.
  • https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jayavarman_VIII, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indravarman_III and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ponhea_Yat: the Shaivite king and his Mongol tribute (1285), Indravarman III as son-in-law and Zhou Daguan's stay of 1296 and 1297, and Ponhea Yat's flight in 1431. The Ponhea Yat page contradicts itself (a reign ending in 1431 or 1463, two accounts of the move, Basan and Chaktomuk), and it names no siege of Angkor.
  • Wikipedia pages for the monuments, each as read: Angkor Wat, Bayon, Baphuon, Ta Prohm, Banteay Srei, Preah Khan, Bakong, Preah Ko, Lolei, Phnom Bakheng, Pre Rup, East Mebon, Ta Keo, Phimeanakas, Neak Pean, Banteay Kdei. Not verified

Written from memory, or not stated by any page read:

  • Realm rings: no page gives borders. They are loose outlines of the lands the pages name (Lopburi, the Mekong basin, Champa, the Kra Isthmus, Vientiane). The ring for Yasovarman I and the early kings is a guess, as are the rings after 1243; the central plain of Thailand under Suryavarman II is the user's brief, not a page.
  • Jayavarman II's route from Java, and the identification of Java with the island: tradition only, and the pages dispute it. The origin pins for Phimai (Vimayapura) and Koh Ker are from memory.
  • Family links: Jayavarman III as the founder's son and Indravarman III as Jayavarman VIII's son-in-law are from the pages; Rajendravarman II's, Jayavarman V's and Udayadityavarman II's relations to their predecessors are not on any page read, so they are left as separate roots.
  • The Baksei Chamkrong claim in a caption (Harshavarman I by one page, Rajendravarman in 948 by another) is reported as the pages give it, not checked further.
  • Monument years that are placements: Phnom Bakheng (about 900), Phimeanakas (1010), the Baphuon (1060), the Bayon (1190), Neak Pean (1191). The coordinates are the pages' own, rounded to four decimals; the pin for the Bayon and the Baphuon sit within a few hundred metres of each other.
  • The Siem Reap River and the road to Roluos on the builder's plan are drawn by eye from memory and are only the rough line of the real ones.

Exclusions

  • Koh Ker (the capital from 928 to 944): about a hundred kilometres from Angkor, so no pin; its kings are folded into the second house and the house says so. Jayavarman IV and Harshavarman II are kept as rulers because the Khmer kings are one line, although Angkor was not their seat.
  • Preah Vihear, Phimai, Beng Mealea and the other temples outside the plain: outside the plan.
  • Angkor Thom (as a pin), the Terrace of the Elephants, Srah Srang, the Baksei Chamkrong, Prasat Kravan, the Thommanon, Banteay Samre, Mangalartha and the Pre Rup's neighbours: left out to keep sixteen pins; Angkor Thom is described in the Bayon blurb because its page gave no coordinates and the Bayon stands at its centre.
  • Udayadityavarman I (about 1001 to 1002) and Jayaviravarman: named on the pages but with no reign dates; the first is not given a reign, the second only as a rival to Suryavarman I.
  • Sithean Reachea, Nippean Bat: two short reigns of 1340 to 1347 in the table, dropped to keep the late house readable.
  • The Chams' occupation of 1178 to 1181 and the gap from 1081 to 1089: real gaps, left open.
  • After 1431: Angkor Wat stayed a Buddhist shrine and the Khmer kings reoccupied Angkor in the 1570s under Barom Reachea I (the Khmer Empire page says he used it, 1566 to 1576), but no page read says a king ruled from Angkor again, so the range ends in 1431; a longer chapter would add Barom Reachea. The French protectorate from 1863 is colonial and excluded.
  • Before 802: Chenla and Funan are described in the pages as predecessors but no king is sourced to the Angkor plain.

Judgement calls

  • Reign years follow Wikipedia's Khmer Empire table, shifted a year where a parent and child, or two houses, would otherwise share a year: Jayavarman III starts 836, Indravarman I 878 to 888, Harshavarman I 911, Ishanavarman II 924, Jayavarman IV 929, Harshavarman II ends 943, Jayavarman V 969, Udayadityavarman II 1051, Harshavarman III 1067, Dharanindravarman I 1108, Suryavarman II 1114, Yasovarman II 1161, Tribhuvanadityavarman 1168, Indravarman III 1296 and Jayavarman IX 1328. Indravarman II is cut to 1242 and Ponhea Yat starts in 1405 (the table's Barom Reachea II), so In Reachea ends in 1404.
  • Suryavarman I starts in 1002 (the infobox), not 1006 (the table) or 1010; the earlier date lets Jayavarman V's house end in 1001 with no gap.
  • Suryavarman II is given 1114 to 1150 against the table's 1113 to 1145, because his own page and the Angkor Wat page give 1150.
  • The houses are slices of a few dynasties, not clean dynasties: the Mahidharapura dynasty runs 1080 to 1336 on its page and is cut into four houses here (Jayavarman VI to Suryavarman II, Dharanindravarman II to the sack, Jayavarman VII's pair and the late kings) to make the court readable.
  • Suryavarman II is a 'nephew' of Dharanindravarman I; the page says great-uncle, so the epithet says great-nephew.
  • The Baphuon is placed under Udayadityavarman II by tradition; the page says recent work puts it earlier, in Suryavarman I's reign.
  • The Phimeanakas was begun under Rajendravarman by one reading and finished by Suryavarman I; it sits under Suryavarman I.
  • Angkor Wat's year is its completion, 1150; the page gives work from 1113 or 1122 to 1150.
  • The range ends in 1431, where Ponhea Yat left, and the house is called Late Angkor; dates for the fourteenth-century kings are one table's.
  • The rings after 1431 are not drawn: there are none.
