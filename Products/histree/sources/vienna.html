Not verified

Written from memory, to check first:

  • Relationships between the later Habsburgs where the source called them something else: Albert V (no parent set), Frederick III (no parent set), Maximilian I as a son of Frederick III, Ferdinand I as a grandson of Maximilian I, Rudolf II and Ferdinand II as grandsons of Ferdinand I, Leopold I as a grandson of Ferdinand II, Franz Joseph I as a nephew of Ferdinand I, and Charles I as a descendant of Franz Joseph (the source says grand-nephew).
  • Babenberg rulers' relationships beyond the list in the source: Leopold IV (taken as a son of Leopold III, since Adalbert II is left out) and Henry II Jasomirgott (a son of Leopold III, the source says brother of Leopold IV).
  • That Vienna itself came under the margraves only in the eleventh century, and not in 976; the first Babenberg frame leaves it outside the ring for that reason. The year 1155 for the move of the seat is from the Vienna page.
  • Hofburg's year of 1260 (the sources say only "the thirteenth century"), the Schottenstift's year of 1155 (the foundation, the church dates from 1160 to 1200), the Plague Column's dates (vowed 1679, unveiled 1693), the Karlskirche's dates (1716 to 1737) and architect, the Prater's opening in 1766, and the Imperial Crypt's year of 1633.
  • The coordinates of every monument, all from memory; the Ringstraße pin is placed by hand on the Ring.
  • Realm rings and region points. Every ring is drawn by eye, and the roughest are the Babenberg margraviate, Ottokar's realm of 1270, and the Habsburg Hungary of 1526.

Exclusions

Before the range:

  • Roman Vindobona (first century) and the Carolingian and Hungarian eastern march before 976. The range starts with Leopold I.

Gaps shown on the rail:

  • 1247 to 1250, between the Babenbergs and Ottokar. It is real: rival claimants held Austria in those years (Gertrude's husbands Vladislaus and Herman of Baden, who never held Vienna in fact).
  • The Habsburgs and the Přemyslids meet in 1276 and 1277, so there is no gap there.

Houses and rulers left out:

  • Babenberg: Burkhard (to 976), Adalbert II (1136 to 1137), and the Dukes of Mödling.
  • Přemyslid: Margaret (the Babenberg heiress who brought Ottokar his claim) and Wenceslaus II (his son, who never held Vienna).
  • Habsburg: about thirty Habsburg dukes who ruled parts of the lands (William, Ernest the Iron, Frederick IV, Sigismund and others), Albert VI, Ladislaus the Posthumous, Rudolf III, Frederick the Fair, Leopold III, Charles V, Maximilian II, Matthias, Ferdinand III, Joseph I. Matthias Corvinus of Hungary held Vienna from 1485 to 1490 and is folded into the Habsburg house (five years).
  • Habsburg-Lorraine: Francis I Stephen (the emperor-consort), Marie Antoinette and the archdukes. Napoleon held Vienna in 1805 and 1809, and the dissolution of the Holy Roman Empire in 1806 is not shown.
  • The First Republic (1918 onward).

Monuments considered and not entered: the Ruprechtskirche, the Michaelerkirche, Maria am Gestade, the Minoritenkirche (where Ottokar's body lay), the Albertina, the Burgtheater, the Rathaus, the University, the Gloriette, the Upper Belvedere's gardens, the Hofburg's Swiss Gate and Imperial Library, the Spanish Riding School's stables, and every Gründerzeit building along the Ring. No cut-off rule was applied beyond a few well-known buildings per ruler, so this list is only what came to mind and is not a survey.

Judgement calls

  • End year 1918: Charles I's reign ended with the dissolution of Austria-Hungary, as the source's list gives it.
  • Start year 976: the first Babenberg margrave, as the Vienna page and the rulers list give. Vienna was not the seat until 1155, but the house is the first one that held the land of Vienna, so it starts the chapter, as Delhi's starts with the Tomars.
  • Hand-off years follow the rule that the next house starts the year after the last ends: Babenberg 976 to 1246; Přemyslid 1251 to 1276; Habsburg 1277 to 1740; Habsburg-Lorraine 1741 to 1918. Reigns keep their real dates within a year.
  • Ottokar's reign starts in 1251, when he entered Austria, though he married Margaret and was formally duke from 1252 and held the title until 1276.
  • Rudolf I's reign starts in 1276, a year before his house; he took Vienna in November 1276 and the Habsburgs hold it from then.
  • The source gives the Habsburg-Lorraine name from Joseph II; I use it for the whole house from Maria Theresa's accession, because Francis Stephen of Lorraine married her in 1736 and the house descended from their marriage. Maria Theresa is listed as the first ruler of Habsburg-Lorraine, though the source files her under Habsburg.
  • Joseph II's reign starts in 1765, when he became co-regent, not 1780.
  • The Plague Column is placed under Leopold I (unveiled 1693). The Imperial Crypt is placed under Ferdinand II (the first burials in 1633, under Ferdinand II), though Empress Anna and Emperor Matthias founded it.
  • The Belvedere is placed under Charles VI with Prince Eugene as patron; the Habsburgs bought it in 1752.
  • St Stephen's is placed under Rudolf IV (1359, the Gothic nave), though the first church was consecrated in 1147 under Henry II and the building ran into the sixteenth century.
  • The Ringstraße is entered as a monument on the day of Franz Joseph's decree (1857).
  • Realm rings do not draw the family's Spanish, Italian and Netherlandish possessions, except for Lombardy and Venetia in 1815.
  • Opening year 1785: the reign of Joseph II and the Vienna of Mozart and Haydn.
