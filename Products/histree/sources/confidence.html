Confidence levels

Notes on src/data/confidence.ts: one entry for each of the 181 houses in the 21 chapters, keyed <cityId>/<dynastyId>, with a level and a one-sentence why (200 characters at most) shown on hover. node scripts/check-confidence.cjs fails if a house has no entry, an entry has no house, or a why is empty or too long, and prints the count per level.

Result: 93 documented, 80 approximate, 4 traditional, 4 contested.

Sources

Each judgement rests only on the per-chapter notes in this folder (the "Not verified" and "Judgement calls" sections) and on the character lines in src/data/<city>.ts. Nothing was looked up again.

How levels were assigned

  • documented: the notes say the house's reigns and hand-offs were checked against Wikipedia lists and agree. A one-year shift made only by the hand-off rule (the next house starts the year after the last ends) does not lower a house.
  • approximate: any of these:
  • the chapter's notes say its dates were written from memory;
  • the notes give two sources that differ by years;
  • the first or last year is a convention (the city's dedication, the year the city changed hands, a regime folded in);
  • the house is a judgement (the Trịnh lords).
  • traditional: the dates come from chronicle tradition, not contemporary records (Baekje's 18 BCE, the Aksumite start and end, the Inca before 1438, the Mexica before 1428).
  • contested: historians dispute who held the city or when (the Zagwe, and the three Timbuktu houses of 1738 to 1893).

The level describes the house's dates and hold on the city, not every ruler or monument in it. Many chapters have unverified rulers' relationships, monument years, rings and origins; those are in each chapter's notes and are not rated here.

Per city

Delhi and Isfahan: every house is approximate. Both chapters were built before sources were recorded, and their notes say "Everything" is unverified. This is the weakest block, and the one most worth checking. If a source pass confirms houses, raise them to documented.

Istanbul: Constantinian is approximate (330 is the city's dedication, not 306 or 324). The Latin house (reigns from memory) and the Ottoman house (sultans' reigns from memory) are approximate. All other Byzantine houses are documented.

Cairo: Ayyubid is approximate (start between 1171 and 1174); Ottoman (a chapter convention, rulers from memory) and Muhammad Ali (ends 1952, the source says 1953) are approximate.

Kyoto: Taira, Kamakura (1184 vs the shogunate's 1192), Ashikaga (control of Kyoto, not the 1338 to 1573 shogunate), Toyotomi and Tokugawa (1600, 1601 or 1603) are approximate.

Beijing: Liao, Jin and Mongol Empire are approximate (hand-offs from memory).

Rome: Valentinian and Theodosian, Odoacer, Ostrogoths (end 536 from memory, the source says 538) and Byzantine (all from memory) are approximate. No early kings are in the chapter, so nothing is traditional.

Córdoba: Jahwarid (inferred years), Abbadid and Almoravid (sources conflict), Almohad (from memory) are approximate.

Vienna: Babenberg is approximate (976 is the first margrave, Vienna came later).

Baghdad: Seljuk (end), Abbasid Restoration (start), Kara Koyunlu (1468 from the brief), Ak Koyunlu, Safavid and Hashemite are approximate.

London: Wessex (Mercian governance 886 to 911) and Denmark (two English-king years folded in) are approximate.

Moscow: Suzdal and Vladimir (1147 is the first chronicle mention) and Daniilovichi (Daniil from 1263 or 1282) are approximate.

Paris: Merovingian (508 vs 509 to 511), Lancaster, Valois restored (both rest on 1436 from memory), Bourbon (League held Paris to 1594), First Empire and Second Empire (republican years folded in) are approximate.

Damascus: Ikhshidid (935 vs 931) and Kingdom of Syria (start convention) are approximate.

Hanoi: Trần (1225 vs 1226), Trịnh, Nguyễn and French Indochina (the 1888 cession rests on one line) are approximate.

Seoul: Baekje is traditional. Goguryeo, Silla and Goryeo are approximate (several Seoul-specific hand-offs from memory).

Axum: Aksum is traditional, Zagwe is contested (regnal lists differ by two centuries and the Zagwe court was at Roha). Solomonic is documented.

Timbuktu: Mali and the Tuareg of Akil are approximate. Tuareg and Kunta (1738 to 1826), Massina and the final Kunta, Tuareg and Tukulor house are contested. Sonni, Askia, Saadi and Arma are documented.

Samarkand: Caliphate, Karakhanid, Khwarazmshahs, Mongols, Timurid, Janid and Manghit are approximate (pages disagree on years, or placeholders).

Cusco: Inca is traditional; the Peruvian Republic is approximate (the 1883 end is chosen).

Mexico City: Mexica is traditional. The rest are documented.

Not verified

  • The whole Delhi and Isfahan blocks (22 houses).
  • That the hand-off convention alone never moves a house by more than one year. The notes say so, but it was not checked house by house.
  • Every "documented" is only as good as the "checked against Wikipedia" claim in that chapter's notes. No source was reopened for this pass.
  • Rome's Odoacer and Valentinian and Theodosian houses and Baghdad's Safavid and Hashemite houses are not named in their chapters' checked lists; they are rated approximate on that silence, not on a known problem.

Judgement calls

  • Axum's Aksum house is traditional, though its character line says "approximate". Its start is a placeholder and its end is the traditional date of Gudit's sack, but kings in between rest on inscriptions and coins. Approximate would also be defensible.
  • Timbuktu's Mali house is approximate, not traditional: the chapter starts in 1324, and the early Mali tradition is outside it.
  • Delhi's Tomar start (736) is probably a traditional date, but the notes do not say so, so it is rated approximate.
  • Contested is for disputes about who held the city or when. The Zagwe qualify because of the two-century gap and the unclear hold on Axum itself.
  • A one-year gap between a house's end and a source's date was left documented; a gap on a house of under about a dozen years (Khwarazmshahs, Kingdom of Syria) was rated approximate.
