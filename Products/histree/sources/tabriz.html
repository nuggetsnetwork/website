Tabriz, Iran

Built: 2026-10-31. Range: 1265 to 1979 (rail 1260 to 1980). Opens at: 1465. Chapter 26. Data: src/data/tabriz.ts, src/data/tabriz-realms.ts.

Tabriz is the fourth chapter for Iran, after Isfahan (the anchor), Tehran and Persepolis. It is the "second heart" of the Islamic centuries: a seat of sovereign power under the Ilkhans, the Turkmen sultans and the first Safavid shahs, and the crown prince's seat under the Qajars. Seven of its ten houses also rule other chapters and are joined to their groups in src/data/links.ts; each blurb and character line says what the house was to Tabriz.

Not verified

  • Pages that returned 404: El_Goli (the title Shah_Goli worked), Ghazan_Khan's_Mausoleum and Shenb-e_Ghazan (the title Ghazan_Mausoleum worked).
  • Ghazan's parentage (son of Arghun) and the Tekuder relation to Abaqa are from the Ilkhans' standard genealogy, not from a page that said so; Tekuder is Abaqa's brother by the page read.
  • The Chobanid origin (Sivas, Erzurum) and the claim that Chupan's house came from Anatolia, the Jalayirid origin from Baghdad as a route, and the Qara Qoyunlu route through Khoy are drawn by judgement.
  • The Timurid split between Miran Shah (1392 to 1399) and Umar (1399 to 1405): the pages give 1392 for Miran Shah and Umar until about 1405 only. The start of Umar is from memory.
  • Alvand's hold on Tabriz until 1501, his relation to Uzun Hasan (grandson), and Ahmad Beg's 1497 reign: from memory beyond the Aq Qoyunlu page's lines. Rustam's reign 1493 to 1497 follows the page's 1497 defeat and Baysunghur's death.
  • Safavid and Qajar reign years are taken from the Isfahan and Tehran chapters' data, not re-read; the 1636 date of the Saheb-ol-Amr Mosque comes from the page's infobox, which contradicts the page's own 1638 destruction.
  • The relation of Naser al-Din, Mozaffar al-Din and Mohammad Ali to the crown prince's seat in Tabriz (that each was crown prince in Tabriz) is from memory; the data lists them as shahs, not governors.
  • The Jameh Mosque's year (1700) and El Goli's (1780) are placements, not dates; the Tabriz bazaar's year (1501) is the start of Safavid capital status, not a building date.
  • Constitution House coordinates are from web search mirrors, not from the Wikipedia page read; the pin may be off by about 100 m.
  • Rings and region points: all approximate. The Chobanid, Donboli and Jalayirid-of-Tabriz rings are drawn by eye; the others reuse Baghdad's, Isfahan's and Tehran's rings. The Mehranrud's last stretch is interpolated.
  • The Hasht Behesht of Uzun Hasan and Yaqub, mentioned in the captions, is not a monument in the data, because no source gave coordinates.

Exclusions

  • Before 1265: Hulagu's Maragheh (1258 to 1265), the Seljuk, Eldiguzid (Atabeg) and Ahrid antecedents, Georgia's capture of Tabriz in 1208, and the Abbasid rebuilding of 791. The pages read give no Seljuk or Atabeg house for Tabriz, and the capital status begins in 1265, so no earlier house was sourced. A longer chapter would add them.
  • Houses folded or left as gaps:
  • Timur's Tabriz 1386 to 1405 is its own Timurid house; the Jalayirid restoration of Ahmad (1406 to 1410, four years) and Qara Yusuf's first capture are left as a real gap, and the Jalayirid house ends in 1385, not 1410 as the brief had it.
  • The Golden Horde in Tabriz (1357 to about 1360) and the Muzaffarid Shah Shuja's months in 1359 are folded: the Chobanid house ends in 1357, the Jalayirid starts in 1358.
  • Puppet Ilkhans and the Jalayirid Hasan Buzurg (1336 to 1337), two years, are left as a gap between the Ilkhanid and the Chobanid.
  • Ottoman occupations of 1514, 1534, 1585 to 1603 (eighteen years, more than the fifteen-year rule would fold), 1635 and 1725 to 1730 are all folded into the Safavid house, because a house id cannot be split in the data: the 1590 frame leaves Tabriz outside the ring. A separate Ottoman house would have needed the Safavid split in two.
  • The Afsharids (Nader Shah, 1736 to 1747) are folded into the Donboli khans' house, who held Tabriz for him.
  • Russian occupations (1828, 1909 to 1917), the Ottoman months of 1915 to 1918, Ismail Nawbari's committee are frame captions, not houses.
  • Rulers dropped: Hulagu, Baydu (Ilkhanids); Yaghi-Basti, Surghan (Chobanids); Hasan Buzurg, who ruled from Baghdad (Jalayirids); Khalil, Murad, Muhammad (Aq Qoyunlu); Pir Budaq and Hasan Ali (Qara Qoyunlu); Ismail II (Safavids); and puppet Ilkhans.
  • Monuments left out: the Hasht Behesht palace and the Uzun Hasan mosque (no coordinates), the Dawlat-khana of Uways (Jalayirid, no coordinates), Ghazan's tomb tower as a standing building, the Saat Tower (the Municipality Palace page used), the Amir Nezam House (no date found), Azerbaijan Museum and Tabriz University (no source read), the old Arg's later military use, the Blue Mosque's own mausoleum (in its blurb). Many other buildings of Tabriz were not read.
  • The Islamic Republic after 1979 is not a house.

Judgement calls

  • The range. The chapter starts at 1265, when Tabriz became the Ilkhanid capital per the Tabriz and Ilkhanate pages, and ends at 1979, where Isfahan and Tehran also end. The Qajar monarchy ended in 1925 but the Pahlavi shahs held Tabriz as a provincial city and lost it for a year in 1946: Isfahan's Pahlavi house is the precedent. A shorter chapter would stop in 1925 and drop the Pahlavi house, its only monument (the Municipality Palace) and its 1946 frame.
  • Hand-offs. Houses start the year after the last ends except where a real gap is left: 1336 to 1337 (Ilkhanid to Chobanid), 1386 is the year after the Jalayirid 1385 end, 1406 to 1409 (Timurid to Qara Qoyunlu), and Qajar 1800 follows the Donboli end of 1799.
  • Abbas Mirza's start is 1801, a year after Fath-Ali Shah's 1800, because a child's reign must start after its parent's.
  • Ilkhanid era begins at 1265 with Abaqa; the Ilkhan list is Abaqa, Tekuder, Arghun, Gaykhatu, Ghazan, Oljeitu and Abu Said. Soltaniyeh became Oljeitu's capital from 1306, and Tabriz stayed the Ilkhanid city; the data does not model the court's partial departure.
  • Jalayirid start is 1358 (the infobox's capital date), though the page gives 1360 for the reconquest of Tabriz.
  • The Jalayirid-Timurid cut shifts Ahmad Jalayir's reign to 1382 to 1385 and a four-year restoration is a gap; Timur's house holds the city 1386 to 1405 and Miran Shah and Umar are its governors.
  • Qara Qoyunlu starts in 1410, Qara Yusuf's capture. The Tabriz page gives 1375 to 1468 for the capital, which no Qara Qoyunlu page supports; the data follows the Qara Qoyunlu page. Iskandar's reign (1421 to 1437) is set from the page's 1421 and 1434 to 1435 events and Jahan Shah's 1438 crowning.
  • Aq Qoyunlu starts in 1468 and ends in 1501, with Alvand last on Tabriz from memory.
  • Safavid span 1501 to 1736 includes the long Ottoman interludes and the move to Qazvin in 1555 (Tabriz still Safavid, but no longer the capital); the 1590 caption says so.
  • Donboli start at 1737, the year after the last Safavid, folds the Afsharid ten years.
  • Qajar start at 1800 (not 1794, the Tabriz page's start of the crown princes' residence): Abbas Mirza entered Tabriz in June 1799 and beat the Donbolis in September 1799.
  • Pahlavi start 1926 follows the Tehran chapter, not 1925.
  • Opening year 1465: the Blue Mosque is finished in Jahan Shah's reign, in the middle of a Turkmen capital; it is a standing monument.
  • Monument placement: the Saheb-ol-Amr Mosque sits under Safi in 1636 although it is said to be Tahmasp's; Ghazan's tomb sits at 1300 (completed between 1295 and 1304); the Rab'-e Rashidi at 1307 under Oljeitu, not Ghazan, because the foundation deed is dated 1307; the Arg at 1310 under Oljeitu; El Goli at 1780 under Najaf Qoli Khan.
  • Confidence. Safavid, Qajar and Pahlavi are documented; the other seven are approximate.
