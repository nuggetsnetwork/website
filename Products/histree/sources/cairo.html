Exclusions

Before the range:

  • Fustat (founded 641 by Amr ibn al-As), the Rashidun, Umayyad and early Abbasid governors, down to Ahmad ibn Tulun's arrival in 868. The range starts at the earliest house sourced for the city as a capital.

Gaps shown on the rail (periods with no house):

  • 906–934: the Abbasid caliphs governed Egypt directly through short-lived governors. This is a real interregnum, not an omission.

Houses and rulers left out:

  • Tulunid: Jaysh (896) and Harun (896–904), the two short-lived sons of Khumarawayh.
  • Fatimid: the caliphs before the move to Egypt (al-Mahdi to al-Mansur), al-Zafir (1149–1154) and al-Fa'iz (1154–1160), child caliphs with no monuments.
  • Ayyubid: al-Mansur (1198–1200), al-Adil II (1238–1240), and the Ayyubid branches in Syria.
  • Mamluk: 8 of the 49 entries on the source list are shown (Baybars, Qalawun, an-Nasir Muhammad, Hasan, Barquq, Qaitbay, Qansuh al-Ghawri, Tumanbay II). Left out: Shajar al-Durr, the sultana of 1250 (dropped to keep the lineage tree compact), and the other 40 entries, including Aybak, Qutuz, Barakah, Barquq's son Faraj, Barsbay, Jaqmaq and Inal. The Bahri and Burji lines are shown as one house.
  • Ottoman: all sultans except five, all the pashas, and the Mamluk beys (Ali Bey, Murad Bey, Ibrahim Bey) as rulers. Muhammad Bey Abu al-Dhahab appears only as the patron of his mosque. The French occupation of 1798–1801 has no house and shows inside the Ottoman era.
  • Muhammad Ali dynasty: Ibrahim (1848), Abbas I (1848–1854), Hussein Kamel (1914–1917) and Fuad II (1952–1953). The British occupation (1882–1952) is not a house; it appears only in the realm captions.

Monuments considered and not entered: the Mosque of Amr ibn al-As (older than the range, rebuilt many times), the Nilometer on Rawda (built 861 under the Abbasids), the Coptic churches and the Ben Ezra Synagogue (not built by a ruling house), the Fatimid palaces and the Great Palaces (no standing remains), many Mamluk and Ayyubid mosques and tombs, the Cairo Tower, the Opera House (burned in 1971), Heliopolis and Garden City. No cut-off rule was applied beyond a few well-known buildings per ruler, so this list is only what came to mind and is not a survey.

Judgement calls

  • End year 1952, not 1953: Farouk abdicated on 26 July 1952 after the Free Officers' coup. Fuad II was nominally king until the republic on 18 June 1953, but held no power, so the house ends in 1952 by the rule "last date a dynasty held sovereign power". This differs from the source's 1953.
  • Fatimid start 969 (the conquest), though the source gives 973 as the year Cairo became the capital. The city's chapter opens with al-Mu'izz, who ruled Egypt from 969 but arrived in 973.
  • Saladin's reign starts in 1171 (when the Fatimid caliphate ended and he held power), not 1174 as the source infobox has it.
  • Hand-off years follow the rule that the next house starts the year after the last ends: Tulunid ends 905; Ikhshidid 935 to 968; Fatimid 969 to 1171; Ayyubid 1172 to 1249; Mamluk 1250 to 1516; Ottoman 1517 to 1804. Reigns keep their real dates within a year. Tumanbay II's reign runs to 1517; Turanshah's to 1250.
  • The Ottoman era ends in 1804 (Muhammad Ali takes power in 1805), though Ottoman suzerainty over Egypt lasted nominally to 1914.
  • The Ottoman origin is marked foreign (Turkey), since Cairo's own land is Egypt. The Istanbul chapter marks the same house "of the land". Both are right for their city.
  • The Mausoleum of Imam al-Shafi'i is placed under al-Adil I (1200–1218), whose reign it falls in, with al-Kamil as patron. Hasan's mosque is placed under him with the year 1363 (completion, two years after his death in 1361); the checker warns about it.
  • Relations: "descendant" is used for Ahmed I, Mahmud I and Abdulhamid I so the Ottoman lineage stays connected after skipped generations. The Mamluk sultans mostly have no family link, so the lineage view shows several roots.
  • Al-Hafiz is shown as grandson of al-Mustansir and al-Adid as grandson of al-Hafiz, skipping the caliphs in between.
  • Cairo's position is the city centre near Tahrir Square, not Fatimid al-Qahira or Fustat.
  • Realm rings take in the sea between a house's coasts, as in the Istanbul chapter.
