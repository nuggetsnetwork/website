Written from memory, to check first:

  • Reigns of Wenceslaus and Charles IV as separate entries (the list gives Wenceslaus as margrave from 2 October 1373 to 1378 and Charles IV as the buyer), Sigismund's two terms as margrave, and the sons and brothers links (Louis II and Otto V as brothers of Louis I; Sigismund as a son of Charles IV; Albert Achilles as a son of Frederick I).
  • The electors' father and son links from John Cicero to George William, and that John Sigismund was a grandson of John George (Joachim Frederick, who is left out, was his father).
  • Frederick William II as the nephew of Frederick II (the monarchs' page says so), and the epithets of most rulers beyond the pages: Joachim I's university at Frankfurt on the Oder, Albert Achilles's inheritance law, Frederick William I's army, Frederick William IV's refusal of the crown of 1849, the Great Elector's army.

Exclusions

  • Before 1237: the pre-city settlements, Albert the Bear (margrave 1157 to 1170) and the first Ascanians; the page says archaeology finds market towns in the late twelfth century. A longer chapter would start with Albert, but no page read puts him in Berlin.
  • The false Waldemar (1348 to 1350): recognised by Charles IV for a time, not a ruler of Berlin in any list read.
  • Joachim Frederick (1598 to 1608): left out of the electors to keep to twelve rulers.
  • Hohenzollern margraves and electors who ruled elsewhere or in the other branches (Brandenburg-Ansbach and Kulmbach), and Albert the Bear's descendants on other lines.
  • The French occupation of Berlin from 1806 to 1808: folded into the kings' house; a regime of under two years.
  • Hindenburg's last year as president (1933 to 1934) and the Reich presidency of Dönitz in May 1945: outside the Weimar house's end, and Dönitz sat at Flensburg.
  • Nazi-era figures other than Hitler and Goebbels (Göring, Speer, Himmler and others): not sovereigns. Speer's plans for a world capital are mentioned in the Tempelhof blurb.
  • Other Allied commandants and the British and French military governors; only a few of the best known are listed.
  • Other mayors of West Berlin (Schreiber, Suhr, Albertz, Stobbe, Vogel, Diepgen) and the East Berlin lord mayor Friedrich Ebert Jr., to keep each house short; and Hans Modrow's colleagues.
  • Monuments left out: the Victory Column (read, but left out to hold the number at sixteen), the Zeughaus, the Altes Museum and Museum Island, Sanssouci in Potsdam, the Kaiser Wilhelm Memorial Church, the Rotes Rathaus, the Berlin Wall Memorial on Bernauer Straße, the Wannsee villa, and the Memorial to the Murdered Jews of Europe (opened in 2005, after the range). The Palace of the Republic (1976, GDR, demolished 2006 to 2009) is not built as its own monument.
  • After 1990: the reunified city, the capital of the Federal Republic, and the sovereignty of the state level, which no dynasty held.

Judgement calls

  • The Hohenzollerns are three houses, not one: the electors (to 1700), the kings (1701 to 1870) and the emperors (1871 to 1918). It is one family, but the realm and the role of Berlin change at each split, so each gets its own house, ring and character. Frederick III (the elector) and Frederick I (the king) are the same man and appear twice, as do William I as king and as emperor (the second is william-i-emperor).
  • The end year: reunification on 3 October 1990 is the last state-level change; the GDR was a sovereign state with Berlin as capital, so it is included to the end. A shorter chapter would end in 1918 (the monarchies) or in 1945 (the Reich).
  • Hand-offs: the Ascanians end in 1320 and the Wittelsbach begin in 1323; the gap is real (no margrave). The Luxembourg house is 1373 to 1415, the Hohenzollern from 1415. The kingdom starts in 1701, the year after the electors' house ends in 1700. The Weimar house ends in 1932 so Hitler's house starts in 1933, although Hindenburg was president until 1934. The Allied house starts in 1946, though the occupation began in 1945, and ends in 1948 with the split of the city; Berzarin, Zhukov and Eisenhower's reigns start in 1945, one year before the house.
  • The Ascanian house starts in 1237, the first mention of Cölln, although John I and Otto III ruled from 1220 or 1231; the reign starts of both brothers are set to 1237. The Brandenburg page says the brothers did not found the towns.
  • The Wittelsbach and Luxembourg margraves are separate houses though short; each is over 15 years.
  • Charles IV is listed as the buyer of the march and a ruler of the Luxembourg house; Wenceslaus has no parent in the data because their reigns start in the same year.
  • William II's parent is William I (grandson), not Frederick III, since Frederick III reigned only from March to June 1888 and the parent must start earlier.
  • Nikolaikirche's year is 1230, an older foundation than the Ascanian house's start of 1237, per the church's page (built 1220 to 1230; the infobox says completed about 1243); the checker warns once.
  • Marienkirche's year is its first mention (1292), not its construction date, which the page does not give.
  • The Soviet War Memorial is placed under Zhukov (1945), and the East Side Gallery under Ulbricht, though the Wall was extended and painted after his time; Charlottenburg is placed under the last elector, Frederick III, who began it in 1695 (as Lietzenburg, for Sophie Charlotte).
  • West Berlin overlaps the GDR for 41 years on purpose: from 1949 two authorities claimed Berlin, one over the east and one over the occupied west. The site's rail shows "two houses claim the city". Its house is marked contested because an occupation is not a state.
  • The Nazi house is told plainly: what the regime was, what it did (the persecution and murder of millions, the war) and what it left or destroyed. It has two rulers because the other leaders were not sovereign. The Reich rings show territory it annexed or occupied by force.
  • The Palace is listed under Frederick II Irontooth (1443); its destruction by the GDR and its copy are in the blurb.
