Hyderabad, India

Built: 2026-11-01. Range: 1591 to 1948. Opens at: 1600. Chapter 31.

Hyderabad is the third chapter for India, after Delhi and Pataliputra, and the first for the Deccan. It adds four houses: the Qutb Shahi sultans who founded the city, the Mughal governors who took it in 1687, the Asaf Jah viceroys of the Deccan who made it their own after 1724, and the Nizams under British protection until Operation Polo in 1948.

Chronology followed

The chapter starts at the founding of the city in 1591, not at the founding of the Golconda sultanate in 1518. Golconda lies 11 km west and was the seat until 1590, so it is described in the blurbs and its fort and tombs stand as monuments of the city, but the Qutb Shahi rulers before 1591 (Sultan Quli, Jamsheed, Subhan, Ibrahim) are not shown. Hyderabad's founding year is given as 1589 or 1591 by Wikipedia; 1591 is the Charminar's year. After 1724 the Asaf Jahs ruled from Aurangabad and the sources give 1763 or 1769 for the move to Hyderabad; the chapter shows them as the lords of the Deccan whose city this became, and says so in the house sentence. The range ends with the Nizam's surrender on 17 September 1948: the state was a princely state under British paramountcy but a state-level sovereign, so the Nizams stay, and a longer chapter would run to 1956, when the Nizam stopped being Rajpramukh.

Exclusions

  • The Qutb Shahis before 1591: Sultan Quli (1518 to 1543), Jamsheed, Subhan and Ibrahim Quli (to 1580), and the 1580 to 1590 reign of Muhammad Quli at Golconda; Hyderabad did not exist, so they are in the monument blurbs only. A longer chapter would start in 1518.
  • Muhammad Ibrahim, the first Mughal governor, removed within two months of the conquest, and the nominal governor Kam Bakhsh's 1700 appointment is kept but short; the interim governors after Yusuf Khan are folded into Ibrahim Khan.
  • Muzaffar Jung (December 1750 to February 1751), not officially recognised; folded into the gap between Nasir Jung and Salabat Jung.
  • The Asaf Jah house is split at 1802 and 1803 (the first Nizam officially styled so), a choice, as the Wikipedia page marks Sikander Jah as the first to be called Nizam; the split also follows Hyderabad becoming the capital in 1763 or 1769.
  • Prime ministers (Salar Jung I and others) and the Paigah nobles: not sovereigns. The Salar Jung Museum (1951) is outside the range. The Spanish Mosque (page not found), Taramati Baradari, the Khairatabad and Toli mosques and the city wall's gates: not read. The Residency: British.
  • The Kalyana-Karnataka and Marathwada areas before 1724; Berar's Nizami years after 1853 are not drawn.
  • The accession of the state to India in 1949, the Rajpramukh years to 1956, and the 1967 death of Osman Ali: after the sovereign end, mentioned only in the notes.

Judgement calls

  • The house boundaries: the Qutb Shahi house ends in 1687 (the conquest) and the Mughals start in 1688, the Mughals end in 1723 and the Asaf Jahs begin in 1724; Mubariz Khan's reign is cut at 1723 though he died in October 1724. Asaf Jah II is set at 1762 to 1802 (his true end is August 1803) and Sikander Jah starts at 1803; the Nizams' reign ends are moved back one year where the next starts in the same year.
  • Muhammad Quli's reign is set to 1591 to 1612, although his reign began in 1580; his father Ibrahim Quli is not in the house, so the parent link is lost and he is a founder.
  • Golconda Fort (1518) and Hussain Sagar (1563) are older than the house and stand under Muhammad Quli as earlier foundations. The Qutb Shahi tombs stand at 1602, the year of his tomb on the page, though the complex spans 1543 to 1672. The Mecca Masjid stands under Sultan Muhammad (1617) though Aurangzeb completed it in 1694.
  • Chowmahalla stands under Salabat Jung at 1750 (infobox), though it was finished between 1857 and 1869. Falaknuma's patron is Viqar-ul-Umra, not the Nizam. The University stands at 1934 (foundation stone), the High Court at 1915 (start) and the Hospital at 1919 (completion).
  • The Mughal house includes Aurangzeb as emperor with overlapping governors, a modelling choice; the governors' reigns overlap each other in 1700 to 1707 (Kam Bakhsh in absence, Rustam Dil Khan in fact).
  • The Qutb Shahis came from the Qara Qoyunlu line, but they are a separate house and are not linked to Tabriz's Qara Qoyunlu house in links.ts. The Mughal governors join the Timurid and Mughal group.
  • The Nizams' state is shown to 1948 as a state-level sovereign under British paramountcy. Sovereignty ended on 17 September 1948 by the data; the page says formal sovereignty ended in 1950 and the title of Rajpramukh lasted to 1956.
  • Thirteen monuments, within the brief's ten to fifteen.
