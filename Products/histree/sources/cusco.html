Cusco, Peru

Built: 2026-10-31. Range: about 1200 to 1883 (extended 2026-10-31 from 1824; see Judgement calls). Opens at: 1500. Chapter 20.

Exclusions

  • Four Hurin Sapa Incas (Sinchi Roca, Lloque Yupanqui, Mayta Capac, Capac Yupanqui): legend; their dates on the Sapa Inca page are traditional; left out to keep the court short. Inca Roca is a root, not linked to Capac Yupanqui.
  • The Killke culture (about 900 to 1200) and before: not a ruling house, no source names rulers. Cusco's own history is older than any house here.
  • Ninan Cuyochi (a few days in 1527), the puppet Incas Tupac Huallpa, Manco Inca (1533 to 1536 in Cusco under Pizarro) and Paullu Inca: folded, under fifteen years and subordinate to the Spaniards; Manco's 1536 to 1537 siege folded into the Habsburg house.
  • The Neo-Inca State at Vilcabamba (1537 to 1572: Manco Inca, Sayri Tupac, Titu Cusi, Tupac Amaru): it never held Cusco, so is not a house; described in the 1572 caption only.
  • The viceroys and governors (Pizarro, Nunez Vela, Toledo and the rest): the crown's officials, not sovereigns; only the kings are listed.
  • Louis I (January to August 1724): folded into Philip V. Joseph Bonaparte (1808 to 1813): folded into Ferdinand VII; nothing read says Cusco obeyed him.
  • Tupac Amaru II (1780 to 1781) and the 1814 rebels: rebellions that did not hold the city long (Tupac Amaru's army was repelled in 1781; the 1814 rising held it from August 1814 to early 1815, about six months), so folded and described in captions. The 1814 Cusco junta was shorter than the fifteen-year rule.
  • Machu Picchu, Pisac, Ollantaytambo: 75 km or more from Cusco and outside the plan, which spans about 4 km.
  • Casa del Almirante (Inka Museum): the Historic Centre page gives Almagro as the first resident and no builder or date; left out unsourced.
  • The Peruvian Republic after 1883: the republic still exists, so the chapter needs a stop; 1883 is the peace of Ancon (see Judgement calls). Earlier text of this file said the chapter stopped with Spanish rule; that decision was reversed on 2026-10-31. A shorter chapter would end in 1533 (the Spanish conquest) and drop three houses and most of the church monuments. Presidents left out of the twelve: Santa Cruz's 1827 Council presidency is folded into his Confederation entry; Gutierrez de la Fuente, Orbegoso (a three-time president and the North Peruvian State's head), Bermudez, Salaverry, Vivanco, Pezet, Garcia Calderon, Echenique-era interims and the 1860s and 1870s others are not listed, each short, a stand-in or given no Cusco connection. The Peru-Bolivian Confederation is folded into the republic house rather than made its own (it lasted less than three years and the pages do not make Cusco its seat). Monuments of the republic: none; no building of 1825 to 1883 in Cusco was sourced, so the house has monuments: [].
  • Bourbon monuments: none; the pages read name no building of 1701 to 1824 in the city, so that house has monuments: []. The Audiencia of 1787 is a body, not a building read.

Judgement calls

  • Start: 1200, as the placeholder said, with the traditional rulers marked; chosen because the Sapa Inca page gives the line and the README rule asks for the earliest sourceable house. A start at 1438 (Pachacuti) would be firmer but would lose the Hurin and Hanan story.
  • Dual kingship: Hurin and Hanan shown as one house; Manco Capac is the only Hurin ruler kept, Inca Roca the first Hanan.
  • Republic house, and the end (2026-10-31): extended to 1883. Reasons: Peru was the sovereign state over Cusco from Ayacucho on, and the project rule is that the last state-level sovereign, not the colonial administration, ends the chapter; Mexico's republic was filled the same day. Cusco was never the national capital: Lima was, and Cusco was one department seat, which the house's character line says plainly. That makes this a weaker fit than Mexico City's and Rome's papacy (where the city was the seat), and the extension is a judgement, not a rule: it could have been left at 1824. The end is 1883 (peace of Ancon on 20 October 1883, ending Peru's war with Chile), not 1879 (the war's start, Pierola's seizure of power on 23 December 1879) and not 1824, because it is the last clearly dated break in the republic's sovereignty, the cession of Tarapaca, that the pages read give; the republic itself did not end there, and a later date (the 1920 constitution, the Leguia coup) would have been as arbitrary and needed a hundred years more of presidents. Peru's republic starts 1825, the year after the Bourbon house ends (Ayacucho 9 December 1824; Bolivar's dictatorship dates from 10 February 1824 on the list, so his reign is shown from 1825). Rulers: 12, all roots, reigns by the list; Gamarra (born in Cusco) 1829 to 1833 with his second term in the epithet; Santa Cruz is placed 1836 to 1839 as Protector though Peru's list has him 1827 as well. Rail end moved from 1850 to 1910 (1883 plus 27 years); the Ages row and the city point era in src/data/cities.ts now end 1883.
  • End until 2026-10-31 was: 1824. The viceroy held Cusco from 1821 to 1824 (it was the royalist capital) and fell at Ayacucho on 9 December 1824; Peru's independence of 28 July 1821 is not an end because Cusco was royalist. The Audiencia lasted to 1825 on its page, not used.
  • Charles I's reign starts 1534, when Spain took the city, not 1516; Ferdinand VII's reign is cut at 1824, not 1833. Habsburg end 1700 and Bourbon start 1701 (Philip V's accession was 1700): the one-year shift keeps the hand-off rule. Philip V's reign runs 1701 to 1746, both his reigns joined.
  • Atahualpa is linked to Huascar as "brother" because the tree links within a house and the true parent Huayna Capac is already linked to Huascar.
  • Qenqo is under Topa Inca, Tambomachay and Puka Pukara under Pachacuti, with no source naming builders; Sacsayhuaman and the Qorikancha under Pachacuti as the tradition says.
  • The Bourbon origin is France (Philip V born at Versailles), the Habsburg origin Spain (the brief), although Charles I was born in Ghent; both are marked foreign.
  • Spanish rings are the viceroyalty's de jure claim, bigger than actual control.
  • Region labels: "Quito" is not used in the 1810 frame (outside the ring).
