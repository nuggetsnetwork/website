Mexico City (Tenochtitlan), Mexico

Built: 2026-10-31. Range: 1325 (traditional foundation of Tenochtitlan) to 1867 (Maximilian shot, 19 June). Updated 2026-10-31: the republic of 1824 to 1863 added as a regime house (see Judgement calls). Opens at: 1519. Chapter 21.

Names: the usual English or Spanish forms, with accents dropped in the data: Mexico City (the data says in a comment that it is the site of Tenochtitlan), Moctezuma (the Tlatoani page's spelling; Montezuma is the old English form), Cuauhtemoc, Cuitlahuac, Ahuitzotl (one page writes Ahuizotl), Axayacatl, Itzcoatl, Huitzilihuitl, Chimalpopoca, Acamapichtli, Tlatelolco, Templo Mayor, Zocalo. Strict Nahuatl spelling (long vowels, saltillo) is not used.

Not verified

  • Dates: Acamapichtli's 1376 is the Tlatoani page's; the brief says about 1375; every date before the Triple Alliance (1428) is traditional. The foundation year 1325 is traditional (the page says the date was chosen in 1925). The Habsburg start 1522 is a judgement (see below). No ruler is placed before Acamapichtli; no reign date was invented.
  • Kinship of the tlatoque is from memory, not from a page read: Acamapichtli as father of Huitzilihuitl and Itzcoatl; Huitzilihuitl as father of Chimalpopoca and Moctezuma I; Axayacatl as Moctezuma I's grandson; Tizoc and Ahuitzotl as Axayacatl's brothers (the Moctezuma II page calls Tizoc and Ahuitzotl his uncles, which supports it); Cuitlahuac as Moctezuma II's brother; the Cuauhtemoc page does support Ahuitzotl as his father. Charles III as Ferdinand VI's half-brother is from memory (the list gives no relation).
  • Wording like "Itzcoatl the first huey tlatoani" is from the Tlatoani page; "levee of Nezahualcoyotl kept fresh water round the city" is from the Tenochtitlan page.

Exclusions

  • Houses before the Mexica: the Tepanecs of Azcapotzalco (overlords, not holders of the island), Culhuacan and Tlatelolco's own rulers (a separate city until 1473; its rulers are not listed and its pyramid is under Axayacatl): outside the range rule.
  • The viceroys and Cortes: governors, not sovereigns; Cortes, who held the city as captain-general from 1522, is shown only as patron of the palace and the hospital. Kings only, as for Cusco.
  • Joseph Bonaparte (1808 to 1813) and Louis I (1724): folded into Ferdinand VII and Philip V.
  • The republic after 1867 (Juarez restored, Diaz and the rest): outside the range; the chapter still ends with the empire's fall. The restored republic held the city from July 1867, so a longer chapter would be defensible, but the existing decision stays. The provisional government of 1823 to 1824 (the Supreme Executive Power, two triumvirates) and the regency of 1863 to 1864 (Almonte, Salas, Labastida, Pavon, Ormaechea): folded or left out, each under two years and not part of the Republic or the Empire house. Presidents left out of the twelve: Bocanegra, the triumvirate of 1829, Muzquiz, Gomez Pedraza, Barragan, Corro, Canalizo, Paredes, Salas, Anaya, Ceballos, Lombardini, Carrera, Diaz de la Vega, Alvarez, Robles Pezuela, Pavon and Bravo (acting terms), each short or a stand-in. Echeverria and the other interim presidents likewise.
  • Moctezuma II's successors after Cuauhtemoc and the later puppet tlatoque under Spanish rule (for example Tlacotzin): no source read names them and they held no sovereignty.
  • The tlatoque before Acamapichtli (Tenoch and the teomama leaders): no page read names dates for them.
  • New Spain's far reaches: the Philippines, the Caribbean, Louisiana, the Floridas and anything north of about 38 degrees are described in the captions or cut, not drawn.
  • Monuments left out: San Hipolito, the Casa de Cortes (no page read), the Plaza of the Three Cultures as a place (it is a twentieth-century plaza; the Tlatelolco site and Santiago church stand for it), the Angel of Independence (1910, outside the range).
  • A shorter chapter ending in 1521 would drop the Habsburg and Bourbon kings, the Iturbide house, Maximilian, and 14 monuments of 16 (only Templo Mayor and Tlatelolco would stay); one ending in 1821 would drop the two Mexican empires, the Reforma, and the rest of the 19th-century story. The 1867 end follows the README rule that foreign dynasties and short empires count.

Judgement calls

  • The house starts: the Mexica era starts 1325 though the first ruler is Acamapichtli, 1376; between the traditional foundation and 1376 the city had no named tlatoani in any page read. The first two frames cover that span, and the checker lists no warning for it.
  • Habsburg 1522, not 1521: the Mexica end on 13 August 1521 (Cuauhtemoc's capture), and the next house starts the year after, which is when Cortes began to rebuild and became captain-general. Charles I's reign is shown 1522 to 1556 for that reason (he was king from 1516).
  • Republic house (2026-10-31): 'house' is a regime or elected office, as for Rome's Republic and papacy. The 'United Mexican States' name and the Federal Republic, Centralist Republic and Second Federal Republic are one house, 1824 to 1863, because the state and the capital stayed the same and the pages give each as a form of the same republic; 12 rulers, all roots (no kinship), reigns from the head-of-state list, with Santa Anna 1833 to 1855 (he held the office eleven times, in turn with others; the span overlaps seven other rulers), Herrera 1848 to 1851 (his 1844 to 1845 terms in the epithet), Bustamante 1830 to 1832 (later terms in the epithet), and Juarez 1857 to 1863 (the liberal line; Zuloaga 1858 and Miramon 1859 to 1860 overlap him as the conservative rival government that held Mexico City, listed by the page as a rival line). Dates: the house starts 1824 (Constitution of 4 October 1824 and Victoria's inauguration), so the First Empire's 1823 abdication to 1824 is covered by the Iturbide house to 1823 and the provisional government is folded in. The house ends 1863 (the French entered on 10 June) and the Second Empire begins 1864 (Maximilian accepted the crown on 10 April 1864): no gap and no overlap, so no change to the empire's dates; the regency of July 1863 to April 1864 is shown in neither house. The old 1824 to 1863 gap on the rail is now filled. The Second Empire's character line was edited: it no longer says the republic years are a gap.
  • Houses: the First Mexican Empire (1822 to 1823, about two years) is its own short house, the House of Iturbide, and so is Maximilian (1864 to 1867, a Habsburg-Lorraine house), not folded. Folding Iturbide into the Bourbons would show a Mexican-born general under Spain's royal colours, and into Maximilian would join two dynasties 41 years apart, so each one stands alone with one ruler, as the Troubles do in Moscow. The 41-year interval between the empires was a real gap until the republic house was added.
  • The Bourbons end in 1821, with independence on 28 September 1821 and the army's entry on 27 September; Iturbide's regency months of 1821 are shown as the end of the Bourbon house, not as Iturbide's. The house of Iturbide starts at his acclamation, 1822.
  • Cuitlahuac (1520) and Cuauhtemoc (1520 to 1521) share a start year but are not parent and child, so the checker is satisfied; Cuauhtemoc's infobox start is 25 January 1521 on his own page, and the Tlatoani page's 1520 is used.
  • Monuments placed by builder or period: the Tlatelolco pyramid under Axayacatl in 1473 (the year of the conquest, not a Tenochca build); the Zocalo, National Palace and Hospital de Jesus under Charles I as of 1522 and 1524; the Old Basilica of Guadalupe under Philip V (finished 1709, begun under Charles II in 1695); Casa de los Azulejos under Philip V for the tiling; the Palacio de Iturbide under Charles III (built 1779 to 1785, before Iturbide used it); Chapultepec Castle under Charles III, with Maximilian's use in the blurb; the Reforma under Maximilian.
  • The Mexica ruler list is 11, all the tlatoque of the Tlatoani page. The other houses are short, so rulers per house are 5, 5, 1 and 1.
  • Rulers' epithets for the early tlatoque say traditional or approximate; the 'about 12 per house' target is met only by the Mexica.
  • The Triple Alliance ring of 1519 and the rest are drawn by eye; the Tlaxcalans and the Tarascans stood outside the Aztec empire but are inside or beside the ring, which the caption says.
  • The plan draws the three causeways and the Reforma as road lines. Lake Texcoco's shoreline is not drawn: no page read gives an outline and most of the lake lies outside the plan frame. The lake appears as a region label on the realm map for the Mexica frames.
  • The city country is "Mexico", as in world.json. The origin of the Bourbons is France (Versailles), as for Cusco.
