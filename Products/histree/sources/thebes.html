Thebes (Waset; modern Luxor), Egypt

Built: 2026-10-31. Range: 2118 BCE to 656 BCE (rail 2140 BCE to 640 BCE). Opens at: 1450 BCE. Chapter 25. Data: src/data/thebes.ts, src/data/thebes-realms.ts.

This is the second Egypt chapter, after Cairo (868 to 1952), and fills the pharaonic millennia before it. The two do not overlap and there is a long real gap between them. Thebes is the Greek name for the Egyptian city Waset; the ruins of Karnak and Luxor Temple on the east bank and the royal tombs and mortuary temples of the west bank are what remain. The chapter counts both banks and the Valley of the Kings as one city. It is a secondary chapter: nine houses, carried by fourteen monuments.

Range and what it leaves out

The range starts with Intef II, the first Theban king the page read says claimed all of Egypt, about 2118 BCE, and ends in 656 BCE, when Psamtik I took Thebes from the Kushite king Tantamani.

The rule is the last date a dynasty or empire held sovereign power over the city. Here that is read as the last house for which Thebes was a seat of power, or contested territory in its own right. After 656 BCE the Saite kings, the Persians (from 525 BCE), the Ptolemies and Rome all ruled Thebes, but from Sais, Persepolis, Alexandria and Rome; the page read says Thebes "never regained its former political significance", only a religious one, and it was razed in the Ptolemaic revolt of 91 BCE. These regimes held Thebes the way the Ottomans held Cairo, so the "foreign houses count" rule would add them. They are left out so that the chapter ends where the city's own history as a capital ends; a longer chapter would add the Saite 26th dynasty, the Persian satraps, the Ptolemies (with the Theban revolts of 132 and 91 BCE) and Roman Thebais, then the gap to the Arab conquest where Cairo's chapter takes over. The Thebes page also gives 29 BCE nowhere; the revolt against Rome that the brief mentioned was not found in the page read, so it is not used.

Exclusions

Before the range:

  • The Old Kingdom and the First Intermediate Period, when Thebes was a small trading post; Intef the Elder, Mentuhotep I and Intef I of the 11th Dynasty, whose reigns the page marks with question marks; Intef II is the first king whose reign it dates.

Gap shown on the rail (an omission, not history):

  • 1801 to 1586 BCE: the 13th Dynasty and the Theban 16th Dynasty. The Thebes page says the 13th Dynasty fled to Thebes after the Hyksos took Memphis and that Thebes then became capital again, so this is a sourced period of Theban rule; no page on the 13th or 16th Dynasty was read and their dates, kings and seat are disputed, so no house was written.

Houses and rulers left out:

  • 11th: Mentuhotep I, Intef I and Intef the Elder.
  • 17th: Sobekemsaf II, Intef and the other kings named on the page with no dates.
  • 18th: Smenkhkare and Neferneferuaten (reigns of one to two years, relation unclear), Ay (1323 to 1319, dropped to keep 12 rulers; the page says he may be Akhenaten's maternal uncle).
  • 19th: Amenmesse (1201 to 1198), who overlaps Seti II and apparently usurped him.
  • 20th: Ramesses VIII (1129 to 1128, one year).
  • High priests: Djedkhonsuefankh (1046 to 1045, one year); the Royal Cache (DB320) at Deir el-Bahari, where priests of this age hid royal mummies, was dropped because no page read mentions it; every high priest of the 22nd Dynasty line, including Iuput, who is named only on the High Priest page.
  • Libyan: Takelot I, Shoshenq II, IV, V, VI, VII, Pami, Pedubast I and II, Iuput I and II, Osorkon IV, Ini and the rest, to keep nine; the Tanite 21st Dynasty (Smendes to Psusennes II), which ruled the Delta, not Thebes.
  • Kushite: Kashta, the first Kushite to reach Thebes, whose dates the page does not give; Piye is shown with a 744 start.

Monuments considered and not entered: the Temple of Seti I at Abydos (160 km north, out of range); Hatshepsut's obelisks and the Red Chapel; the Temple of Mut (404); the tomb of Seti I (KV17) and Ramesses III's tomb (KV11), named on the Valley page but not entered; Seti I's own mortuary temple; the Temple of Khonsu; the Malkata palace of Amenhotep III; the Valley of the Nobles; the Chapels of the God's Wives of Amun at Medinet Habu (named on the page, 8th to 6th century BCE); Medinet Habu's Small Temple of Hatshepsut and Thutmose III.

Judgement calls

  • The Twelfth Dynasty is a house although Thebes was not its seat. The page read lists both Thebes (Manetho's claim) and Itjtawy as capitals, and it held sovereignty over Thebes while building at Karnak. Under the rule that foreign and distant houses count (Ottomans in Cairo), it counts. Dropping it would leave a gap from 1990 to 1585 BCE.
  • The Ptolemies, Persians and Romans are not houses, though by the same logic they could be: see Range above.
  • Dates: one chronology is stated per house and followed as printed on the Wikipedia dynasty page read (which follow no single scholar). Eleventh: Intef II 2118 to 2069 to Mentuhotep IV 1991 (two sets on the page; the one with the kings' reigns). Twelfth: the table's rounded dates, shifted by a year so each handover is the next year (1990 for Amenemhat I, which the page gives as 1991). Seventeenth: the page's succession box start of 1585. Eighteenth to Twentieth: the dynasty pages' tables. Kushite: 744 from the lead.
  • Co-regencies are ignored: Hatshepsut and Thutmose III both start in 1479 BCE (her de facto date, his page date); Amenhotep II starts in 1425 though the page gives 1427, to follow Thutmose III's end.
  • The Twentieth Dynasty ends in 1081 BCE although Ramesses XI reigned to 1077, because Herihor's rule over Thebes began in 1080; the priests are the house for the city from then.
  • The Libyan house runs 943 to 745 although the 22nd Dynasty's table starts Shoshenq I in 947 or 945, to follow the Third Intermediate Period page's 945 or 943 for the reunification and start after the high priests' end. The Tanite kings who never ruled Thebes are not rulers of this house (only Shoshenq I, Osorkon I, Osorkon II and Shoshenq III, who put their sons or kin at Thebes), and the Upper Egyptian kings of the 23rd Dynasty rule Thebes in their own right. The dates use the 23rd page's Kitchen model; the page gives an alternative.
  • Karnak is placed under Senusret I as the founder (c. 1970 per the page), not under a New Kingdom builder; the New Kingdom parts (the Hypostyle Hall, the Festival Hall of Thutmose III, the Kiosk of Taharqa) are their own entries with their builders. The year is outside his reign by eight years because of his co-regency.
  • The 18th Dynasty's Akhenaten has no monuments: he is shown at Amarna (the capital moved there in his fifth year), not at Thebes.
  • Thutmose I is credited with the Valley of the Kings, though the page says burials may start under Amenhotep I; the year is a placeholder.
  • The Great Hypostyle Hall is credited to Seti I loosely: the Karnak page says most new building was by Seti I and Ramesses II and that the hall "may have begun during the Eighteenth Dynasty".
  • The colossi's year, 1352 BCE, is within Amenhotep III's reign (which the page ends in 1351) while the page says completed about 1350.
  • Relation "descendant" is used where the pages give no exact relation but the ruler belongs to the house.
  • Origins of the Theban houses (Armant, Edfu) are placeholders for a Theban start; Thebes was a small town before the 11th Dynasty.
