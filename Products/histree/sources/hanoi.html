Exclusions

  • Before the range: the Chinese rule of Giao Châu, Đại La under Gao Pian (866), the Ngô, Đinh and Early Lê houses ruled from Cổ Loa and Hoa Lư, and the Lý Nam Đế of the sixth century. None held Hanoi as a seat, or none is sourceable at the site. The range starts when Lý Thái Tổ made the city the capital.
  • Dương Nhật Lễ (1369 to 1370), the Trần adopted heir overthrown in a year: dropped to keep the Trần house to twelve rulers. Lý Thẩm and Lý Nguyên Hoàng, Trần Cảo, Mạc Toàn and the Cao Bằng Mạc (1592 to 1677, who did not hold Hanoi), Lê Túc Tông (six months in 1504), Lê Chân Tông, Lê Duy Phường and Lê Thuần Tông, Lê Ý Tông, Lệ Đức Hầu and other minor emperors of the Early and Restored Lê: dropped for length or because they held nothing. Trịnh Kiểm (1545 to 1570) and Trịnh Cán (two months in 1782): Kiểm did not hold Thăng Long, Cán was a boy of two months. Nguyễn Nhạc (Thái Đức, 1778 to 1788), emperor at Qui Nhơn: not a ruler of Hanoi; Huệ held it. The Nguyễn emperors Dục Đức (three days), Hiệp Hòa (six months), Kiến Phúc (eight months): dropped for length; Hàm Nghi (1884 to 1885) is kept because of his flight. Đồng Khánh stays because the Turtle Tower is his. The Later Trần pretenders Giản Định Đế and Trùng Quang Đế (1407 to 1414) never held Hanoi.
  • The Nguyễn lords of the south (1558 to 1777): never held Hanoi.
  • Hồ and Ming have no monuments in the data: the Hồ built their citadel in Thanh Hóa, and the Ming left nothing standing in Hanoi that the sources read name. The Trần and the Mạc have no surviving building the sources read give; the Trần-era Hanoi temples on the Buddhist-temples list had only "constructed during the Trần dynasty" and no year. The Lý Quán Thánh and Bạch Mã are tradition.
  • Monuments left out: Đồng Xuân Market (1889 and French), the Tonkin Palace group beyond the Governor-General's Palace, the Hanoi railway station and other colonial buildings, Láng Pagoda (the page gave no coordinates and conflicting dates), Thê Húc Bridge and the Pen Tower (no sourced date or builder), the Imperial Citadel's Đoan Môn gate (the page dates the standing five-gate structure only to "the Lê dynasty"), the Hoàn Kiếm Lake itself, the Old Quarter as a whole.
  • The French colonial years are now the French Indochina house (1889 to 1945). Left out of it: Constans (16 November 1887 to 22 April 1888) and Richaud (1888 to 1889), who served before the house starts; every acting governor (Bonhoure, Picquié, Luce, van Vollenhoven, Monguillot, Graffeuil, Silvestre and the rest); Rousseau, Doumer's predecessor; Roume, Long, Baudouin, Merlin and others between Sarraut's two terms and Varenne; the Japanese commanders after 9 March 1945 (Tsuchihashi, Tsukamoto), Bảo Đại's Empire of Vietnam (March to August 1945) and its imperial delegate in Tonkin, and the Allied occupation after August 1945. Sarraut's two terms are one ruler (1911 to 1919).
  • The Nguyễn emperors Thành Thái (1889 to 1907), Duy Tân (1907 to 1916), Khải Định (1916 to 1925) and Bảo Đại (1926 to 1945) were removed from the house when it was cut to 1888: they reigned after Hanoi was ceded, from Huế, and putting them under the French Indochina house would present them as governors. They are named in the Nguyễn character line and in Judgement calls. Đồng Khánh is kept with his reign cut from 1889 (his death, December 1889) to 1888.

Judgement calls

  • The range, and the 1888 cut (revision of 2026-10-31). The chapter starts in 1010, the year of Lý Thái Tổ's move to Thăng Long, not in 1009, the year of his coronation at Hoa Lư. The first version ended with the Nguyễn on 25 August 1945; the README rule now stops a range at the end of the last state-level sovereign, with one exception for a capital ruled directly by a foreign power, and Hanoi is that exception, so the range still runs to 1945 but the Nguyễn house now ends in 1888. I chose 1888 over 1887 or 1883 to 1884: the Hanoi article says that "since 1888" the city was a French concession ruled directly, whereas the Treaties of Huế (1883 and 1884) made Tonkin a protectorate in which the emperor still nominally governed Hanoi, and 1887 is only the formation of the Indochinese Union (17 October 1887), which did not itself take the city. The French Indochina house therefore runs from 1889 (the year after) to 1945. St Joseph's Cathedral (1886) and the Turtle Tower (1886) fall in the last two Nguyễn years, under Đồng Khánh, though the French allowed or built them: both date from the protectorate, before the cession. Đồng Khánh's reign is cut from 1889 to 1888 for the house; Thành Thái, Duy Tân, Khải Định and Bảo Đại went on reigning as figureheads from Huế but are removed (see Exclusions) because the house they belong to no longer governed the city. French Indochina and 1945. It is a regime house of governors-general (roots, no kinship), with Decoux last and the Japanese coup of 9 March 1945 (which ended French rule and set up Bảo Đại's Empire of Vietnam until the August Revolution) folded into the house's last frame instead of being a house of its own: Japan's control of the north from September 1940 was already the condition under which the French governed, the Empire of Vietnam held Hanoi for five months, and the Japanese commanders are not sourced well enough to list. The house is foreign: true, from Paris. A shorter chapter would end in 1888. A shorter alternative: end in 1888, when the Nguyễn court ceded Hanoi to France as a concession and the city stopped being under any Vietnamese authority at all; or in 1883 to 1884, when the protectorate treaties took away the emperor's government. The Nguyễn rings from 1884 show only Annam and Tonkin, the two protectorates; the French Indochina rings (Vietnam, Cambodia and, from 1899, Laos) are hand-drawn like the rest.
  • The hand-off years. The next house starts the year after the last ends, so several starts moved against the sources: Trần 1226 (the list gives 1225; the Trần page body gives 1226), Hồ 1400 (the Trần end is moved back to 1399, though Thiếu Đế reigned to March 1400, so that Hồ Quý Ly's 1400 and his son's 1401 are in order), Ming 1408 (the conquest was in 1407), Early Lê 1428, Mạc 1528 (Đăng Dung proclaimed himself on 15 June 1527 and the Early Lê ends in 1527), Restored Lê and the Trịnh 1593 (Hanoi retaken in December 1592), Tây Sơn 1789 (Nguyễn Huệ was proclaimed emperor in 1788 but took the city from the Qing in January 1789) and Nguyễn 1803 (Hanoi taken on 20 July 1802, Gia Long's reign shown from 1803). Rulers' reigns are shifted to match; Quang Trung's reign starts 1789 and the list says 1788, Mạc Đăng Dung's 1528 and the list says 1527, and Trần Nhân Tông's is 1279 (the list) where the Trần page says 1278.
  • Hồ and Tây Sơn as short houses. The Hồ (seven years) and the Tây Sơn at Hanoi (1789 to 1802, thirteen) are under the fifteen-year limit. I kept them as houses, as the Moscow chapter did with the Troubles, because neither neighbour could carry them: folding Hồ Quý Ly into the Trần would present a usurper as a Trần emperor, and folding the Tây Sơn into the Trịnh and Lê or the Nguyễn would do the same with a regime that was at war with both.
  • The Ming as a house. Twenty years of direct Chinese rule (1408 to 1427) is over the folding limit, so the Ming are a house with the three emperors of the time, Yongle, Hongxi and Xuande. The governors and generals (Zhang Fu, Chen Qia) are not rulers in the data. The Ming are the only foreign house (foreign: true, country "China").
  • The Lê and the Trịnh. The Trịnh lords held real power from 1545 but Hanoi only from 1592. I made them a house of their own (1593 to 1787) and kept the Lê emperors as a second house (1593 to 1788) with the same dates, so the two overlap for 195 years, the "two houses claim the city" case in the rail. This is true to the history: the emperor and the lord both had a court in the city. The overlap makes the checker warn. The alternative would have been one house of both lineages, with too many rulers. The gap flagged between the Trịnh (1787) and the Tây Sơn (1789) is not a gap in the city, since the Lê house runs to 1788.
  • Lê Thần Tông is one ruler from 1619 to 1662 though he reigned twice (1619 to 1643 and 1649 to 1662, with Chân Tông between); the epithet says so.
  • Placing the monuments. Quán Thánh and Bạch Mã are under Lý Thái Tổ at 1010 because the tradition ties them to the reign; no page gave a year. The Imperial Citadel is under Lý Thái Tổ at 1010 although every later house rebuilt it. Kính Thiên is under Lê Lợi at 1428, the page's year; Lê Thánh Tông finished it, as the blurb says. Trấn Quốc is under Lê Kính Tông at 1615: the page gave the move to 1615 but no ruler. Kim Liên is under Trịnh Sâm at 1771: the page says "a Trịnh lord" in 1771 and 1771 is in Sâm's years (1767 to 1782). Ô Quan Chưởng is under Trịnh Doanh at 1749 (memory). The Đống Đa mound is under Quang Trung: the battle, not the 1851 mound, is dated 1789. The North Gate (1805) is under Gia Long: the page gives 1805 for his rebuilding; the gate may be a later Nguyễn addition. The Flag Tower (1812) is under Gia Long, but the page says 1805 or 1812 and names no ruler, and 1812 is inside his reign. Ngọc Sơn is under Tự Đức at 1864, the repair by Nguyễn Văn Siêu: the pages disagree on the temple's own date (eighteenth or early nineteenth century). The Turtle Tower is under Đồng Khánh at 1886; it was built by Nguyễn Hữu Kim under French influence, an intermediary of the Nguyễn and French governments, a case that the rule allows only because the Nguyễn were still the nominal sovereigns.
  • The One Pillar Pagoda and the Imperial Citadel are not original: the pagoda was blown up in 1954 and rebuilt, and the citadel is mostly archaeological. The blurbs say so.
  • Plan. The builder's plan frames the Red River, West Lake and Hoàn Kiếm Lake (all three drawn with the river kind, since the types have none for a lake) and fits to the chosen ruler's monuments, as Damascus does. The Lý Thái Tổ plan therefore zooms on the citadel, the temples and the two guardian shrines.
  • The frame is [92 to 120 E, 7 to 26 N], taller than the usual 28 by 20 degrees' proportion since Vietnam runs from 8.5 to 23.4 N.
  • Names. Vietnamese spelling and diacritics are used throughout; the court initials will show the first letter of each ruler's name (L, T and so on), so the Lý, Lê and Trần repeat. The id fields are ASCII.
  • Placing the French-era monuments. Doumer (13 February 1897 to 14 March 1902): Hỏa Lò at 1901, when the prison was finished, though it was begun in 1896 under Rousseau, and the Long Biên Bridge at 1902 (the works ran 1899 to 1902 and it opened in 1903). Beau: the Governor-General's Palace at 1906, finished under him though begun in 1900 under Doumer. Sarraut: the Opera House at 1911, the opening on 9 December 1911 (Klobukowski's term had ended in February 1911 with Luce acting until Sarraut in November), although it was begun in 1901 and mostly built under Beau and Klobukowski. All are placed where the checker wants the year inside a reign and may mislead on who commissioned them. The palace is today the Presidential Palace of Vietnam.
  • Term years.
