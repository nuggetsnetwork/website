Exclusions

  • Everything before 665: the Kushans, Kidarites and Hephthalites, and the Nezak kings. The Tepe Maranjan monastery (fourth to sixth century) is kept as an earlier foundation under the first Shahi king, as is the Bala Hissar (about the fifth century) under Babur.
  • Bo Fuzhun and Pati Dumi (Turk Shahis; dates unknown, and Bo Fuzhun may be Khingala), Lalliya (identity disputed with Samantadeva) and Trilochanapala and Bhimapala (they ruled after Kabul was lost): left out.
  • The Samanids (about 875 to 999): no page read says they held Kabul, so they are not a house.
  • The Qarlughids, Kartids and Qara'unas, and the Ilkhans themselves as rulers: named on the Kabul page but without dates for Kabul, so the Mongol house shows Khwarazmians, Mongol khans and Chagatai khans only.
  • Muqim Arghun (1502 to 1504): the Timurid page lists him as a usurper; folded into the interlude by ending the Timurid house in 1503 so that Babur starts the Mughals in 1504.
  • The Safavids: no page read says they held Kabul (they held Kandahar), so there is no Safavid house and no link.
  • The Hotaks (1709 to 1738): they held Kandahar and Isfahan, and no page read puts them in Kabul.
  • Shah Shuja's British-backed return (1839 to 1842), Fateh Jang and Shahpur Shah (1842): under 15 years, folded into the Barakzai house, whose realm frames note it. Nasrullah Khan (February 1919, a week) and Habibullah's end-of-reign Habibullah Kalakani (nine months) are the only short reigns shown; Inayatullah (three days), Ali Ahmad Khan and the 1929 Amanullah return are left out.
  • Sulaiman Shah, Abbas Mirza and Ali Shah (Durrani; weeks or months), the Durrani brother Humayun at Kandahar, Mohammad Afzal and Azam (Barakzai, one year each) and Ayub Khan (1879 to 1880): curation.
  • The republic of Daoud (1973 to 1978), the PDPA and Soviet years (1978 to 1992), the Mujahideen government and the Taliban emirate (1992 to 2001), the Islamic Republic (2001 to 2021) and the Taliban from 2021: not built. See Judgement calls for why the chapter ends in 1973. A longer chapter would add them as regime houses.
  • Monuments: Wazir Akbar Khan Mosque, Chehel Sutun in Paghman, the Sher Darwaza wall (the Bala Hissar page covers it) and Mir Masjidi: no page read gave their dates and coordinates. The Bamiyan Buddhas are 230 km away and left out. Minaret of Jam is not in Kabul.

Judgement calls

  • The end in 1973. The README says a dynasty's range ends when it loses sovereign power; Zahir Shah was deposed on 17 July 1973 and the king abdicated in August. A longer chapter, to 2021, would add regime houses (as Berlin did for the GDR) but no page read covers those years, and the 1992 civil war and the Taliban years are better done with their own sources. Flagged for the user.
  • The Saffarids folded. Ya'qub took Kabul about 870 and his governor held it until about 879 or 880, but the Hindu Shahis had it back by 900: under 15 years, so he is a ruler of the Shahi house, marked as a usurper, with a realm frame at 870.
  • The Shahis as one house, 665 to 976. The Turk Shahis (665 to 822) and the Hindu Shahis are two dynasties by the page, but only one kingdom of Kabul; the end in 976 is set before the Ghaznavid start of 977. The dates are traditional (al-Biruni) and conflict on the pages.
  • The Ghaznavid start at 977 is Sabuktigin's accession; the Kabul page says he took Kabul "around 977". The Ghaznavid house ends in 1186 with the end of the dynasty although the Ghurids probably held Kabul earlier, from some date not given in any page read.
  • The Ghurid start at 1187, a hand-off, is a judgement, and the house is marked contested.
  • The Mongol house is a loose chain: Muhammad II, Jalal al-Din, Genghis, Chagatai, Duwa, Esen Buqa and Tarmashirin. Which of them actually held Kabul at which date is not clear from the pages read; the realm frames say so. Genghis's start is 1221 and Chagatai's 1228 (the page gives 1227) to keep the parent reign ahead of the child's; Muhammad II is set at 1216 to 1220, the house's first year.
  • The Timurids, 1370 to 1503: Timur starts the house with his own throne year; no page gives the year Kabul came under him. Abd al-Razzaq's single year is shortened to end in 1503.
  • The Mughals' Humayun reign 1530 to 1556 covers both his own reigns; Kamran, who held Kabul separately from 1530 to 1553, overlaps him. Mirza Hakim is set at 1575 to 1585 though the page gives 1575 to 1580 as an infobox span and his death in 1585.
  • The Afsharids are kept as a house of one ruler though they held Kabul under a decade (1738 to 1747); Nader's reign is set at 1739 to 1746 to fit between the Mughals' end in 1738 and the Durranis' start in 1747. The README's under-15-years rule would fold them; the user's brief named them, so they stay.
  • The Barakzai split. The dynasty is one family; the emirate (1824 to 1918) and the kingdom (1919 to 1973) are two houses because the realm changes at independence. Sultan Mohammad's 1823 is moved to 1824 for the hand-off. Dost Mohammad's two reigns are one entry, 1826 to 1863, with the 1839 to 1843 interval in the caption. Habibullah's end is set at 1918 though he was shot in February 1919.
  • The ring of the emirate is the modern outline (the Durand Line, 1893) from 1863 on, which overstates the emirate in 1863 and ignores the 1880 to 1919 protectorate; the captions say so.
  • Bala Hissar and the Gardez Ganesha placement. The Bala Hissar sits under Babur at year 500, the Tepe Maranjan under Barha Tegin at 400: both are earlier foundations. The Ganesha is under Khingala, the king named in its inscription.
  • Open year 1927: Darul Aman's completion and the capital's modern years show the most buildings; it is a choice.
