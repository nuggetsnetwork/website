Not verified

  • Dates: the Umayyad and Abbasid governors are only three men (Qutayba, Nasr ibn Sayyar, Abu Muslim), and the pages do not say that Nasr or Abu Muslim governed Samarkand itself, only Khurasan and Transoxiana; the house is the caliphate's. 818 as its end is the year before Nuh ibn Asad's 819. Nasr ibn Ali's own reign dates (the Karakhanid ruler given as one year, 1000) were not read. Ya'qub Qadir Khan 1089 to 1095 overlaps Ahmad ibn Khidr's 1081 to 1089 end and the executed-in-1095 note. Khalil Sultan's holding Samarkand 1405 to 1409 is from memory (the page only gives his reign and a scroll made in Samarkand). Amir Husayn's 1364 start and the Abu Sa'id (Shaybanid), Iskander and Pir Muhammad I dates are as the Shaybanids page lists them; Pir Muhammad I and Iskander are given as one block.
  • Relations from memory: Qara Hülegü and Alghu as Chagatai's grandsons were read, but Yesü Möngke as Chagatai's son, Baraq as a descendant, Du'a as Baraq's son and Tarmashirin as Du'a's son are from memory; the Shaybanid Abu Sa'id as Kuchkunju's son, Ubaydullah as Shaybani's nephew and Abdullah II as Iskander's son; the Janid brother, nephew and father-son links (the Janid page says its genealogy is unclear).
  • Monument years, coordinates and blurbs: Rukhabad's 1380 is the brief's (the page says fourteenth century only); Abdi Darun's 1430 is a placeholder for "the first half of the fifteenth century under Ulugh Beg"; Shah-i-Zinda's 1372 is the Turkan Ago Mausoleum, not the whole necropolis; the Afrasiab year 700 is a placeholder; the Observatory is dated 1429 against 1428 on its own page; the Hazrat Khizr builder is unnamed on the page and is placed under Nasrullah by date. Afrasiab coordinates are the museum's.
  • Realm rings, region points, origins: every ring is by eye; origin coordinates for Balkh, Termez, Gurganj, Balasagun, Tashkent, Otrar, Karakorum, Bukhara, Kesh, the steppe, Astrakhan, St Petersburg and Orenburg are from memory. The Samanid origin (Saman near Balkh, Afghanistan, with Tajikistan as the other claim) was not on the pages read. The Isma'il conquest of Khurasan from the Saffarids (caption of 900) and the Gur-Khan's vassalage (caption of 1141) are from memory.

Exclusions

  • Everything before 712 (the Sogdian city, the Achaemenid satrapy, Alexander's Maracanda in 329 BCE, the Sasanians, the Kidarites, the Hephthalites and the Gokturks): before the range; no house of Samarkand's rulers can be sourced from the pages read.
  • The Tahirids (nominal governors of Khurasan over the early Samanids), Rafi ibn al-Layth's revolt of the 800s and Harun al-Rashid and al-Ma'mun: not on the pages read; folded into the caliphate's house or not named.
  • Samanid Ibrahim ibn Ilyas, Ilyas and Yahya ibn Asad (they ruled Herat and Shash, not Samarkand), Ibrahim ibn Ahmad (947, months), Abd al-Aziz (992), Abd al-Malik II (999 to 1000) and Isma'il Muntasir (1000 to 1005): folded into the Samanid house, the last as an unsuccessful attempt to restore it.
  • The Qara Khitai (1141 to 1212) are not a house: the Karakhanids stayed as their vassals, and the page read says they remained in power. The Seljuks (1089 to about 1141) the same.
  • Karakhanid Khidr (1080 to 1081, a year) and the unnamed khans of 1000 to 1040 and 1156 to 1178.
  • Mongol Qara Hülegü's second reign, Mubarak Shah, Buqa Temur and Kebek, Yesun Temur and the Ogedeids: not Samarkand-specific; Chagatai khans who ruled elsewhere; the Sarbadars of Samarkand (1365, named only in a Registan sentence) are not on the pages read.
  • Timurid Shah Rukh (ruled from Herat; Ulugh Beg governed Samarkand under him), Babur's seven months (1497) and his two other brief holdings, Ali Mirza and the other Samarkand princes of 1497 to 1500: folded into the Timurid house.
  • Shaybanid interlude 1510 to 1512 (an uncle, a cousin and a brother; Babur held Samarkand in 1511 to 1512 from memory), Nawruz Ahmed, Abdullah I and Abd al-Mu'min (1598, months).
  • Nadir Shah's raid and overlordship of 1740 to 1747 and the Afghan and Persian overlordships: folded into the Janid house, as the Janids stayed on the throne. Muhammad Rahim's 1747 to 1753 and 1756 to 1758 dates conflict (see Judgement calls). Manghit Husayn (1826 to 1827) and Umar (1827, months): folded into Haydar and Nasrullah.
  • The Soviet era from 1917 (Uzbek SSR capital 1925 to 1930; no dynasty).
  • Monuments left out: the Afrasiab Museum itself (a building of 1970), Shaybani Khan Madrasa (the page gives only a 1504 record and no surviving building I could place), the Zarafshan water bridge, the Ibrahim Tamghach madrasa and the Karakhanid citadel palace (no surviving building), the Siab Bazaar and the Chorsu dome, the Russo-Chinese Bank, and Ak-Saray (it is in Shahrisabz, not Samarkand). Khoja Abdi is Abdi Darun and is in.

Judgement calls

  • Start at 712 with "the caliphate's governors" as a house, because the README rule counts foreign houses that held the city (the Umayyads and Abbasids, as in Damascus and Baghdad); a shorter chapter would start at 819 with the Samanids and drop the Arab conquest and the first century of Islam in Samarkand.
  • End in 1917, by the rule, with the Romanovs: a shorter chapter would end in 1867 with the last local emir and drop Russian colonial rule (the railway of 1888, the Russian city, the church of 1916). The Romanov house is colonial rule by a dynasty; the three emperors are in as the sovereigns, though none visited, and the governors-general (von Kaufman and after) are not.
  • Hand-offs: Manghits end 1867 and Romanovs start 1868 (the city fell in 1868, so Muzaffar's reign is cut at 1867 and the 1868 loss is the Romanovs' first year). The Samanids end 999 and the Karakhanids start 1000; the Karakhanid first ruler, Nasr ibn Ali, is a one-year placeholder (1000) because no reign was given. The Khwarazmshahs start 1213, though the page says Muhammad II retook Samarkand in 1212, because Uthman was executed in 1212 and the Karakhanid house ends there.
  • The Khwarazmshahs (1213 to 1219) are seven years, under the rule's fifteen, and are kept as a house against the folding rule, because Muhammad II moved the capital to Samarkand and the brief asked for the house. Flagged for the user to decide.
  • The Khwarazmshah origin is Gurganj, which is Konye-Urgench in Turkmenistan, so foreign: true with "Turkmenistan"; the brief proposed Uzbekistan and foreign false. Khwarazm as a region is shared by Uzbekistan and Turkmenistan.
  • Janid and Manghit hand-off: the Samarkand page says Janids 1599 to 1756 and Manghits 1756 to 1868; the Khanate and Emirate pages say the Manghit ataliq Muhammad Rahim murdered Abu al-Fayz in 1747 and rules from 1747 to 1753. The chapter follows the Samarkand page (Janid era to 1755, Manghits from 1756) and lets nominal Janid khans (to 1753) cover the gap. The same page and the Emirate page disagree on Rahim's years; his reign is cut to 1756 to 1757 and Daniyal Biy starts 1758.
  • The Samanid capital: the Samanid page says Samarkand was the capital from 819 to 892 and Bukhara after; the Samarkand page says the Samanids made Samarkand their capital 875 to 999. The Samanid page was used.
  • The Timurid end is 1500 and the Shaybanid start 1501 by the Samarkand page (Uzbek warriors took control in 1500, Shaybani took the city in 1501). The Timurid page puts the Samarkand and Bukhara losses by 1501 and the empire's end in 1507 (Herat); the brief's placeholder end of 1507 is dropped.
  • Timur's start: the Chagatai page dates his control from 1369, the Timurid page his founding to 1370 and says he took Samarkand in 1366; the chapter uses 1370, as the brief does, and ends the Mongol house in 1369.
  • Reign starts shifted by a year where the page gives a shared year or the child would equal the parent: Chagatai 1228 (page 1225 or 1226, after Genghis's 1227 death; he held the ulus earlier), Ahmad ibn Asad 842 (page 841), Nasr I 865 (864), Isma'il I 893 (892), Baraq 1267 (1266), Qara Hülegü 1243 (after 1242), Yesü Möngke 1247, Vali Muhammad 1606, Imam Quli 1612, Nadr Muhammad 1643, Abd al-Aziz 1646, Subhan Quli 1681, Ubaydullah 1703, Abu al-Fayz 1712, Haydar 1801, Muzaffar 1861, Alexander III 1882, Nicholas II 1895, Abu Sa'id (Shaybanid) 1532, Ubaydullah (Shaybanid) 1535, Abdullah II 1584, the Karakhanids' Ya'qub Qadir 1090.
  • The Shaybanid Pir Muhammad II is one year (1598); the page gives 1598 to 1599. Abdullah II starts 1584 (page 1583) so that he starts after his parent's reign began; the 1583 realm frame still carries the page's year.
  • Timurid relations: Ulugh Beg is Timur's grandson through Shah Rukh, who is not in the data, so Ulugh Beg links to Timur directly; Abdullah Mirza is Ulugh Beg's nephew (his father Ibrahim Sultan is Ulugh Beg's brother, from memory) though the page says only that he is Abdal-Latif's cousin.
  • Monuments under a ruler who did not build them: Tilya-Kori under Abd al-Aziz (the page has no patron; the Janid page names Yalangtush Bahadur); Abdi Birun under Imam Quli (1633 work by Nadir Devanbegi); Abdi Darun under Ulugh Beg with a placeholder year; the Ishrat Khana under Abu Sa'id as the work of his consort; the Hazrat Khizr under Nasrullah by date. Afrasiab is placed under Qutayba though it is older than the chapter.
  • The plan uses the Siyob and the Dargom canal from OpenStreetMap, smoothed by eye, and an arc for the Afrasiab hill drawn by eye; the Zeravshan itself is several kilometres north and is off the plan.
  • openYear 1428: Timur's and Ulugh Beg's buildings are up, and the observatory is finished.
