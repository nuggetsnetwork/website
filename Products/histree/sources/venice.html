Exclusions

  • Doges not shown: about 70 of the 120. The data keeps founders, builders, turning points and the famous, at most 12 per house.
  • The marked-legendary doge Marcello Tegalliano (717 to 726): left out with the other doges before Orso Ipato's 726.
  • Two "interludes" that sit between houses: Orso Orseolo (regent 1030 to 1032) and Domenico Orseolo (1032), and Pietro Barbolano (1026 to 1030), are folded into the early-doges house's end and not listed.
  • Napoleon and his Kingdom of Italy (1805 to 1814): not a house, since it ran about nine years; it is folded into the Habsburg-Lorraine house in the data and told in the 1806 frame's caption.
  • The Republic of San Marco of 1848 to 1849: under two years, not a house.
  • Savoy and Italy (1866 to 1946): the chapter ends in 1866 when Venetia passed to Italy; a longer chapter would add the House of Savoy, which is already Rome's last house.
  • Monuments left out for lack of a sourced page: Santa Maria dei Miracoli, Ca' Foscari, the Basilica dei Santi Giovanni e Paolo, the Zecca and the Libreria, the Scuola Grande di San Marco, the Ponte dei Sospiri and the Ghetto.
  • Pre-range: the Roman and exarchate lagoon, and the traditional founding of 421 (the history page notes no surviving record).

Judgement calls

  • The Byzantine duchy (697 to 810) is a house of the local dukes, not of the emperors. It is deliberately not linked to Istanbul's Byzantine houses: the emperors were distant overlords, and this chapter's rulers are the doges.
  • Where a house ends and the next begins: the commune ends in 1413 and the Serenissima begins in 1414; the Serenissima ends in 1570 and the late republic starts in 1571. These are choices, since the republic never changed its constitution there. The 1414 start follows Michele Steno's end; the 1571 start is Lepanto, and so Alvise I Mocenigo's reign starts in 1571 although he was elected in 1570.
  • The 697 start is tradition; the list marks Anafesto and Tegalliano as legendary. The gap from 737 to 742 (the annual commanders) is real, as is 755 to 764, with Galla Gaulo and Domenico Monegario left out.
  • Obelerio's reign ends in 810 by the hand-off rule; the doge list gives 811.
  • Houses are regimes, not families: the doges are elected and are mostly roots in the lineage; only true father and son or brother pairs (Orso and Teodato, the Galbaios, the Orseolo, the Participazio) are linked.
  • The Habsburg-Lorraine house covers 1798 to 1866 though French rule held the city from 1805 to 1814; Francis's reign is shown unbroken and the caption says so. It is linked to the Habsburg-Lorraine group as an Austrian province, a role the other members do not share.
  • Cyprus: the history page gives 1473, the territory page 1489; the ring adds Cyprus in 1489.
  • Monument years: the Doge's Palace is dated 1424 (Foscari's extension, while the building began about 1340); Ca' d'Oro 1430 (inside the 1421 to 1437 build); the Campanile 1514 (the belfry; the first tower is of the 10th century); St Mark's 1063 (the first church of 829 stands under the earlier house); Torcello 1008 (rebuilding; the foundation is 639); the Arsenal 1104 (the page says there is no evidence for the year); the Salute 1631 (groundbreaking; the vow was in 1630 under Nicolò Contarini, not under Erizzo); San Giorgio 1566 (the monastery dates from 982).
  • The Redentore and the Salute list the Senate as patron: both were votive churches of the state.
