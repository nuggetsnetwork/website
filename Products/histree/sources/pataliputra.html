Pataliputra, India

Built: 2026-11-01. Range: 490 BCE to 1763. Opens at: 250 BCE. Chapter 30.

Pataliputra (modern Patna) is the second chapter for India, after Delhi. It adds nine houses: the Magadhan capital of the first empires, a long real gap while the city shrank, and the Patna of Sher Shah, the Mughals and the nawabs of Bengal.

Chronology followed

Dates before about 320 BCE are approximate and are printed from Wikipedia's dynasty pages, which follow the Buddhist (Mahavamsa) chronology; the Jain and Puranic lists, and a later "short chronology", move the Haryanka, Shishunaga and Nanda kings by decades. The Maurya dates are the infobox dates of the Maurya Empire page, whose own text gives Ashoka's accession as 268 or 272 BCE (the data uses 268). Years before the common era are shown as BCE.

Not verified

  • Dates: the Haryanka, Shishunaga and Nanda reigns inside the houses are set by the hand-off rule from Mahavamsa years; Mahapadma's years are the page's infobox; the Nanda reigns between 340 and 330 are an omission. Kalashoka's, Nandivardhana's and the Shunga kings' relations are from the pages' tables but the Puranic and Buddhist lists conflict.
  • Relations from memory: Shuja-ud-Din as Murshid Quli's son-in-law, Sarfaraz as Shuja-ud-Din's son, Farrukhsiyar as Bahadur Shah I's grandson, Daud as Sulaiman's son (a footnote on the Karrani page says so), Purugupta as Skandagupta's brother (the Gupta page says agnate brother).
  • Mughal Bihar: the 1575 start (Akbar took Patna in 1574 per the Patna page; the data starts the year after the Karrani loss of the city), Parviz Mirza as a governor of Bihar (the mosque page names only Parviz Mirza as builder), the 1704 renaming under Azim-us-Shan, and Patna as a "governor's seat" are from the Patna page or from memory, not the Mughal pages.
  • Patna's holders from 1555 to 1564, and when the Karranis held Patna before 1574, are not in any page read; Sher Shah's founding of Patna as a "fort town" is from the Patna page, which gives no year; the Sur and Karrani pages do not mention Patna.
  • Monument years are placements inside a reign except the mosque (1540 to 1545, from its page), the Pather Ki Masjid (1621), the Yakshi's findspot (1917) and Guru Gobind Singh's birth (1666). The Agam Kuan is placed at 260 BCE and the Kumhrar hall at 250 BCE as the Mauryan guess; the Yakshi and the Lohanipur torso are disputed (Mauryan or Kushan and later) and sit under Ashoka only as the older reading.
  • Pather Ki Masjid has no coordinates in the page read; its position is placed by description, near the Ashok Rajpath and the Takht, from memory.
  • Origins, routes and every ring: from memory and by eye. The Mughal rings are copies of Delhi's. The Taxila origin of Chandragupta rests on a tradition not read here; the Gupta origin is described as disputed because the Gupta page says so. The region point for Magadha is a placement near Gaya.
  • Alexander's army at the Beas, Seleucus's cession, Kalinga, the Bactrian Greeks, the Marathas' hold on Orissa (the Bengal Subah page gives 1752), the Company's retaking of Patna in 1763: from memory or one line of a page.

Exclusions

  • The Kanvas (about 73 to 28 BCE): the Kanva page says the Puranas put them at Pataliputra but their coins come mainly from Vidisha, so they are left out and the chapter has a gap from 74 BCE to 318. A longer chapter would add them as a contested house.
  • The Satavahanas, the Mitra dynasty of Kaushambi and the Kushans: claimed to hold Magadha between the Kanvas and the Guptas, but no page read places any of them at Pataliputra.
  • Rulers dropped to keep houses to about twelve: Bimbisara (ruled from Rajagriha), Darshaka (folded into Nagadasaka), Kunala (blinded, never ruled), Shatadhanvan and Devavarman among the later Mauryas, Ramagupta and Kacha (uncertain identities), Kumaragupta II and III, Ghatotkacha and Sri Gupta, and Bhagabhadra and the other Shungas, Taj Khan and Bayazid Karrani, Firuz and the other short Sur rulers, the Mughal emperors between Farrukhsiyar and 1717.
  • The Palas and the other houses that held Bihar between 550 and 1538: no page read places any of them at Pataliputra or Patna (the Pataliputra page says no Pala-period evidence has been found there; Xuanzang found a village in 637). The gap from 551 to 1537 is real. Houses of the Delhi Sultanate that held the Patna country are not in the data for the same reason.
  • Sher Shah's fort, the Padri ki Haveli (1772) and the Golghar (1786), the Jalan Museum's Qila House (1919) and the Khuda Bakhsh Library (1891): the first is gone, the others are British or later and lie after the end of the chapter.
  • The East India Company's rule from 1764 (diwani in 1765, annexation in 1793) and Bihar and Orissa Province (1912): colonial administration, and the chapter ends where the last nawab held the city.
  • A Mughal governor list for Bihar: no page read gives one.

Judgement calls

  • Nine houses, one more than the brief's top of eight: Haryanka and Shishunaga hold different dynasties' capitals; the Mughals and the nawabs are split at 1717 because the nawabs governed as a separate regime.
  • The house starts at 490 BCE from the infobox date of Ajatashatru's fort, not from Udayin's founding; the Haryanka rulers' reigns follow the Mahavamsa, with Ajatashatru's start moved from 492 to 490.
  • The Sur house folds the Karrani Afghans, and sits in a long ruler gap (1554 to 1565); the end at 1574 is Akbar's reaching Patna (Patna page), not the 1576 Karrani end.
  • The Mughal house starts at 1575, the year after the Patna page's 1574. Farrukhsiyar's reign is cut at 1717; the nawabs' Murshid Quli Khan starts at 1718, a year after the page's 1717.
  • The Gupta house runs to 550 although Ayodhya was a capital after 455 and the Pataliputra page says Kumhrar was abandoned about 600; chosen as the line's last recognized king, and the page's disestablishment (about 579) is not used.
  • The nawab house ends at 1763 with Mir Qasim's fall from Patna, not Plassey (1757) or Buxar (1764); Mir Jafar's second reign (1763 to 1765) is left out.
  • Guru Gobind Singh's birth (1666) stands for the Takht, whose present building was rebuilt in 1948 to 1957; the Yakshi and the torso are under Ashoka although their dates are disputed.
  • Monuments: eight, not ten to fourteen; the brief's other names were checked and left out above.
