Fez, Morocco

  • Realm rings: no page gives borders. They are loose outlines of the places the pages name (Fez, Walili, Sijilmasa, Aghmat, Tlemcen, Tunis, Zaragoza, Aoudaghost, the Niger bend). The Idrisid, caliphal, Wattasid and Dila'i rings are guesses.
  • Origins: that Berber tribes at Walili took Idris I as their imam, Volubilis' coordinates, the Chelif valley as the Maghrawa homeland, Tinmel, the Draa valley, Figuig as a Marinid waypoint, Rissani and the Middle Atlas point are from memory. The Hejaz origin of Idris I and the Alaouites is on the pages.
  • Idris II's accession (791, 792 or 803) and the relation of Abu Yusuf Yaqub to Abu Yahya (brothers, both "ibn Abd al-Haqq") rest on the lists; Abd al-Haqq II's father is left out.
  • Moulay Ismail as brother of Moulay al-Rashid and Muhammad III as Ismail's grandson (through Abdallah) are from the list of monarchs.

Exclusions

  • The Idrisid rulers Ali II, Yahya III and the later Idrisids of the north (al-Qasim Jannun, Ahmad, al-Hasan II): they held the north under Fatimid or Umayyad overlordship, not Fez, in pages that say little about the city.
  • Banu Ifran and Hammadid capture of Fez (1032, 1062), and Buluggin's 985 return: folded into the Maghrawa and caliphal houses.
  • The Idrisid sharif Muhammad al-Jutey (1465 to 1471): a real gap on the rail, six years, not folded.
  • Ali Abu Hassun (1549 to 1554) and the brief Wattasid return of 1554: a Wattasid claim after the Saadis held Fez; the Wattasid house ends in 1548.
  • The later Alaouites (Yazid, Abdallah, the sons of Ismail in the 1730s): no monuments and a confused sequence; the sultans after Muhammad III are curated.
  • Monuments left out: Sahrij Madrasa (no page), the Nejjarine fountain (the page says only the funduq is eighteenth century and gives no coordinates), Bab Chorfa (no page), Dar Dbibegh, the Jnan Sbil gardens, the Moulay Abdallah Mosque, Kasbah Cherarda, the Andalusian-quarter and Qarawiyyin libraries as separate sites.
  • The French protectorate from 1912 and independence in 1956: colonial; the Alaouite house stood as nominal sovereign but the executive passed to the Resident-General, so the chapter ends at the Treaty of Fez. A longer chapter would run to 1956.
  • Almoravid Abu Bakr ibn Umar and the later Almoravids; Almohad caliphs after 1224; Marinid sultans of 1358 to 1420 except Abu Salim: not carried in the data.

Judgement calls

  • The chapter ends on 30 March 1912, the Treaty of Fez, and not in 1956, because the README rule stops at the last state-level sovereign and colonial administration is excluded. Abd al-Hafid signed and abdicated; the sultans remained as figureheads. Flag: a longer chapter would run to 1956, and the monarchy still reigns, so the rule for constitutional monarchies is not applied.
  • The Fatimid and Umayyad overlords are one house (927 to 987) because the pages describe one contested period; its rulers are men attested for single years (927, 933, 956, 973, 985 to 987), so each has a reign of one year or two. The house is joined to both the Umayyad and Fatimid groups.
  • Idris II's reign starts in 792 so that it differs from his father's end (791); Hasan I's reign ends in 926 so that the caliphal house starts in 927; the Maghrawa house starts in 988, with Ziri, although the Fes page gives 980 or 986 for the dynasty.
  • The Almohad end (1249) and the Marinid start (1250) follow the Fes page; the Almohad page puts Marinid suzerainty at 1248.
  • The Saadi house ends in 1640, a year before the Dila'i capture of Fez; the Dila'i house runs 1641 to 1665 on one ruler, whose span is partly a guess, and the Alaouites begin in 1666.
  • Monuments placed in a ruler's reign that did not build them, or whose date is a guess: the Andalusian Mosque under Ahmad ibn Abi Said (956, the minaret; founded 859 to 860), the Chouara Tannery under Dunas (a placement; tradition says the city's founding), Bab Bou Jeloud under al-Mansur (1195, a placement; the 1913 gate is outside the range), Bab Guissa under al-Nasir (1212; an older gate dates from 1059 to 1061), the Marinid Tombs under Abu Salim (the first listed burial, 1361), the Mellah under Abd al-Haqq II (1438, from the tomb story of 1437), Dar Batha under Hassan I (1886; finished 1907 by Abd al-Aziz). The Qarawiyyin is placed under Yahya I at 859 although its major enlargements are Almoravid and Marinid.
  • Borj Nord and Borj Sud are both dated 1582 by their pages.
  • The caliphal ring and the Idrisid rings are guesses; they are marked so in the captions.
