Reign years in the list page for Ayyubid and Mamluk Damascus are used as given except where Judgement calls say otherwise. al-Mu'tamid as a son of al-Mutawakkil is from memory (the list page called him a "cousin", inferred). Burid parentage (Buri son of Toghtekin, Isma'il, Mahmud and Muhammad sons of Buri, Abaq son of Muhammad), as-Salih Isma'il and al-Kamil as sons of al-Adil I, and as-Salih Ayyub as al-Kamil's son are from memory; the pages read did not state them. an-Nasir Yusuf as a great-grandson of Saladin is from memory.
  • Epithets and blurb facts from memory: Yazid I, Mu'awiya II, Sulayman's fleet, 'Umar II, Yazid II, Hisham and Rusafa, al-Walid II, al-Mutawakkil's plan to move his capital to Damascus, al-Qahir's blinding, Khumarawayh's recognition by the caliph, Isma'il and Banias, Mahmud's murder, as-Salih Isma'il's sale of castles, Selim's winter in Damascus, the 1148 siege lasting four days, Timur carrying craftsmen to Samarkand, Abdul Hamid II laying the Hejaz Railway, Mehmed VI's grave at the takiyya (the Wikipedia page did say that).

  • Jand's position and the Seljuks' point of origin, Merv as a stand-in for the Burids, Fergana for the Ikhshidids and the whole Ayyubid origin (Dvin or Tikrit) are uncertain; the origin of Toghtekin was not given beyond "Oghuz Turkic".

  • Region points in the Levant (aleppo, lebanon, hauran, transjordan, bekaa) are estimates.

Exclusions

Before the range:

  • Aram-Damascus, the Assyrian, Persian, Hellenistic, Roman and Byzantine periods, and the Rashidun conquest and governorship (634 to 661; Mu'awiya was governor of Syria from about 639 but the caliphs ruled elsewhere): the chapter starts with the Umayyad caliphate's seat, 661, as the brief recommended. A shorter chapter would drop everything before the Abbasid takeover; a longer one would start with the Rashidun conquest in 634 or the Romans in 64 BCE.
  • The French Mandate (1920 on) and independent Syria: not a dynasty.

Houses and interludes folded:

  • Mongol rule of Damascus in 1260 (under Kitbuqa, a few months): folded into the Ayyubid close and the Mamluk start.
  • Timur's sack in 1400 to 1401: folded into the Mamluk house and shown in the 1400 frame caption.
  • Ibrahim Pasha's Egyptian rule, 1832 to 1840 (eight years): folded into the Ottoman house, noted in the 1831 caption. The Tankiz Mosque blurb mentions the barracks.
  • The Qarmatian and Turkish warlord rule of 973 to 983 (Alptakin, Qassam al-Turab) and Ibn Ra'iq (939 to 942) and Sayf al-Dawla (945, 947): each under 15 years, folded into the Fatimid and Ikhshidid houses.
  • Qutuz's weeks in 1260, Sunqur al-Ashqar's 1279 revolt and Ghazan's occupation 1299 to 1300: folded into the Mamluk house.
  • Tutush II and Irtash (1104): months, folded into the Seljuk end. Ibrahim and Yazid III (744): months, left out of the Umayyad list.

Rulers:

  • Umayyad: Ibrahim ibn al-Walid and Yazid III (744), dropped for months of reign. Abbasid: al-Muntasir, al-Musta'in, al-Mu'tazz and al-Muhtadi (861 to 870), four short reigns dropped to keep the house to eleven. Tulunid: Jaysh (896, months). Fatimid: governors listed on the List of rulers of Damascus (about forty), only the conquering general Ja'far is kept. Burid: the regent Mu'in ad-Din Unur (named in Abaq's epithet). Ayyubid: al-Adil II (1238 to 1239), as-Salih Ayyub's first months in 1239 and as-Salih Isma'il's 1237 reign (folded into the neighbours). Mamluk: Solamish, Kitbugha, Lajin, Baybars II and the Bahri sultans from 1341 to 1382 (Hasan and the others), Jaqmaq's and Barsbay's neighbours, Tuman Bay II (1516 to 1517, did not hold Damascus). Ottoman: all sultans not listed (Selim II, Mehmed IV and the rest) for the 12 limit; Mehmed VI (July to October 1918).
  • The Ottoman and other governors of Damascus (the Azm family, Midhat Pasha and the rest) are not rulers here.

Monuments:

  • Not added because no Wikipedia article or no readable source was found: Bimaristan Arghun, Dar al-Hadith al-Ashrafiyya, Madrasa al-Jaqmaqiyya, the Madrasa Nuriyya (Nur ad-Din's tomb, not read), Khadra Palace (Umayyad, no building survives), Darwish Pasha Mosque (1574, patron known, but the page names no sultan so the ruler could not be fixed), Hamidiyah Souq, Maktab Anbar, the Mariamite Cathedral, Bab Touma, Bab al-Salam and the other gates. The Roman Temple of Jupiter is part of the Umayyad Mosque's own blurb.
  • Straight Street is a plan feature and not a monument.

Judgement calls

  • Hand-offs: each later house starts the year after the earlier house ends when they share a year, as the rule says. So Abbasid starts 751 (taking in 750; al-Saffah's reign starts 751 though he was caliph from 750), Burid 1105 (Toghtekin took the city in 1104), Zengid 1155 (Nur ad-Din took it in April 1154), Ayyubid 1175 (Saladin took it in November 1174), Mamluk 1261 (September 1260), Ottoman 1517 (autumn 1516), Hashemite 1919 (Faisal's government dates from 5 October 1918). The Ottoman house ends 1918 (the last governor, Shukri Pasha, 1 to 2 October 1918). The 1917 date in the brief and the 1913 on the page read: see the Hejaz station below.
  • The Abbasid house ends 877 and Tulunid 878 to 905; the Abbasid restoration is its own house (906 to 934), since the Tulunid gap of 27 years is over the folding limit. al-Mu'tamid's reign is cut to 877 where he was caliph to 892.
  • The Abbasid and Fatimid houses are treated as holding Damascus whoever the governor was, so their rulers are the caliphs. The Ikhshidids start 935, the year the dynasty is dated from, not the earlier governorship the list page gives to Muhammad ibn Tughj (931 to 935, under the Abbasid restoration).
  • Saladin's reign is 1175 to 1193 (his death) and al-Afdal's 1193 to 1196, not the List of rulers of Damascus's 1174 to 1186 and 1186 to 1196, which treat al-Afdal as viceroy from 1186; the sovereign was Saladin until he died in Damascus in 1193. al-Adil I follows from 1196 (the list's dates). The rest of the Ayyubid dates are the list's, with as-Salih Isma'il's 1237 reign folded.
  • The Umayyad house ends 750 though Damascus was no longer the capital after 744 (Marwan II ruled from Harran). The list page counts him.
  • Marwan I is a second root in the Umayyad lineage tree (a different branch of the family, elected at Jabiya). Marwan II is listed as a "descendant" of him; Umar II is a "nephew" of 'Abd al-Malik (his father 'Abd al-'Aziz was 'Abd al-Malik's brother; from memory).
  • Qalawun is linked to Baybars as "usurper" because he took the throne from Baybars's son Solamish; al-Ghawri is linked to Qaytbay as "usurper" though he was Qaytbay's mamluk and not a successor in line. The Mamluks are many roots (Barquq, Barsbay and Qaytbay start new ones), and a 41-year run of sultans between an-Nasir Muhammad and Barquq is skipped.
  • The Ottomans' parent links are real kinship but not always successive (Murad III is a grandson of Suleiman, Ahmed III a descendant of Murad IV, Selim III and Mahmud II both grandsons of Ahmed III).
  • Monument years and houses: the Umayyad Mosque is dated 715 (completed; begun about 706). The Citadel is placed under Atsiz with 1076 although al-Adil's rebuilding from 1203 gave the present form, and the blurb says so. Bab Sharqi is placed under Nur ad-Din with the year 200 (the Roman gate; the warning "built 200, outside the reign" is intended), since the source read gives his alteration but no date. The Hejaz station is placed at 1913 as the page says, under Mehmed V; the brief gave 1917 and that was not found in the page read. The Tekkiye is dated 1554 (first year of the 1554 to 1559 mosque). The Dome of the Treasury is placed under Harun al-Rashid at 789, built by the governor Fadl ibn Salih, as most sources say (from the page: "most commonly" late 8th century). The Zahiriyya is under Baybars at 1277 though finished about 1281 under Qalawun, with Barakah as patron. The Adiliyya is dated 1215 (founded; the page gives no completion date).
  • "Country" for origins: Umayyads and Hashemites from Mecca (Saudi Arabia), Abbasids from Humayma (Jordan, as in the Baghdad chapter), Tulunids from Samarra (Iraq; the family itself was from Bukhara), Ikhshidids from Fergana (Uzbekistan), Fatimids from Mahdia (Tunisia), Seljuks from the steppe by the Syr Darya (Kazakhstan), Burids from Merv (Turkmenistan, a stand-in), Zengids from Mosul (Iraq), Ayyubids from Tikrit (Iraq) with Dvin (Armenia) noted, Mamluks from Cairo (Egypt), Ottomans from Istanbul (Turkey).
  • Frame: [24..52 E, 24..43 N]; the Umayyad and Abbasid realms extend past it (the map frame is the country plus margin and the rings are clipped there).
  • openYear is 715, the year of the Great Mosque's completion in the Umayyad peak.
