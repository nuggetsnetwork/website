Kraków, Poland

Built: 2026-11-01. Range: 1000 to 1918 (rail 960 to 1950). Opens at: 1520. Chapter 38. Data: src/data/krakow.ts, src/data/krakow-realms.ts. Kraków is the anchor chapter for Poland.

The scope choice

Kraków was the seat of Polish rulers from 1038 until the court moved to Warsaw in 1596, but it stayed the coronation and burial city (the last coronation at Wawel was in 1734) and its Wikipedia page notes Warsaw was designated capital officially only in 1793. So the chapter does not stop in 1596. It covers every house that held sovereignty over Kraków, from the first Piast hold in the year 1000 (the bishopric founded at the Congress of Gniezno) to the end of Austrian rule in 1918. The Second Republic, the German occupation of 1939 to 1945 and the People's Republic are left out: they are not dynasties, and the README rule ends a chapter at the last dynasty or empire to hold sovereign power. A longer chapter would run on to 1989 and would need regime houses and a few monuments (Nowa Huta, Schindler's factory).

The kings who ruled from Warsaw after 1596 are kept as houses (Vasa, the native kings, Wettin, Poniatowski) because Kraków was still the legal capital and coronation city and they held sovereignty over it. They are the thinnest part of the chapter: few built anything here.

Exclusions

Before the range:

  • The Vistulan settlement, Great Moravia (876 to 879), the Bohemian duke Boleslaus I (955) and Mieszko I's takeover of Kraków near the end of his reign: before the first Piast hold I could place (1000, the bishopric).

Houses and periods left out or folded:

  • The Přemyslid kings of Bohemia (Wenceslaus II, duke of Kraków 1291 to 1305, and his son Wenceslaus III, to 1306): about fifteen years, folded into the Piast house as a gap between Henry IV Probus (1290) and Władysław I (1306). This is a borderline case for the fifteen-year rule and could be made a house.
  • Henry III of Valois (1573 to 1574): a year, folded into the gap before Báthory.
  • Stanisław Leszczyński (1704 to 1709, 1733 to 1736 as claimant): short and contested, folded into the Wettin house.
  • The Duchy of Kraków's other high dukes: Władysław II the Exile (1138 to 1146), Bolesław IV the Curly, Mieszko III the Old, Władysław III Spindleshanks, Konrad of Masovia, Henry II the Pious (1238 to 1241), Bolesław II the Horned, Leszek II the Black (1279 to 1288), Przemysł II (1290 to 1291), Mieszko IV Tanglefoot: dropped to keep twelve Piasts and the arc readable.
  • The Piast reigns of Mieszko I, Mieszko II Lambert and Bretislav's raid of 1038, and the gap from 1026 to 1037: before Casimir I made Kraków the capital.
  • The presidents of the Free City's Senate after Wodzicki (1831 to 1846) and the dictator of the 1846 uprising: not named on any page read, so left out; the Free City house therefore has only two rulers and a gap in its ruler lineage, though the house itself is continuous.
  • The Austrian governors of Galicia and the presidents of the Kraków Fortress: not rulers.
  • After 1918: the Second Polish Republic, the Nazi occupation and the General Government with Hans Frank at Wawel, the People's Republic to 1989, the Schindler factory and Nowa Huta. The range rule ends the chapter at the last dynasty or empire to hold sovereign power.
  • Monuments: Sigismund Bell (a line in the Wawel Cathedral blurb instead), the Kraków Arsenal (the Czartoryski Museum blurb names it), Collegium Novum, St. Anne's Church, the Cloth Hall's 19th-century arcades, the Jagiellonian Library, the Lajkonik and the Hejnał are mentioned or left out to keep eighteen.

Judgement calls

  • The Anjou house starts in 1371 though Louis was crowned in November 1370, so the Piast house can end in 1370 and the next house start the year after. Jadwiga is placed in the Anjou house (she was the daughter of Louis) with a reign of 1384 to 1385, though she reigned to 1399; from 1386 her husband begins the Jagiellon house and the data stops her where it starts. The epithet says so.
  • The Piast house begins with Bolesław I in 1000 even though the capital moved only in 1038: the 1000 date is the bishopric of Kraków. Casimir I's reign is set from 1038 (his page says duke from 1040, the Kraków page says 1038 for the seat of government).
  • Casimir II the Just is given 1177 to 1194 although the Seniorate page's table says 1191 to 1194; the text of the same page says he took the throne in 1177.
  • Henry the Bearded is set 1232 to 1238 (the Duchy page); the Seniorate page's table shows him briefly in 1225.
  • The elected kings are split into houses by dynasty or family, one for Báthory and Vasa, one pairing two unrelated native nobles (Wiśniowiecki and Sobieski) as "native kings" with relation "founder" for both, Wettin, and Poniatowski. Houses with one ruler (Báthory, Poniatowski) are thin but carry real turning points.
  • Báthory begins in 1573 though he reigned from 1576, so the house starts the year after Jagiellon ends. His ruler reign is set 1576 to 1586.
  • The Duchy of Warsaw (1809 to 1815) and the Free City (1815 to 1846) are one regime house with Frederick Augustus I of Saxony and Stanisław Wodzicki as rulers; neither is a dynasty. The 1846 end and the 1847 start of the Habsburg-Lorraine house are separated by one year because Austria annexed on 16 November 1846 and the next house starts the year after the last ends.
  • Franz Joseph's reign is set 1849 to 1916 (he succeeded in December 1848) and Ferdinand I's 1847 to 1848 so as not to overlap or duplicate the year.
  • Monuments are placed under the ruler in whose reign the dated work falls (Remuh 1557 under Sigismund II Augustus; Skałka's Baroque rebuilding of 1733 under Augustus III though its legend belongs to Bolesław II in 1079; Wawel Cathedral's 1320 under Łokietek though consecrated in 1364 under Casimir III; St. Andrew's 1098 under Władysław Herman, who was the duke, while the palatine Sieciech built it). Kazimierz is a district, not a building.
  • The Old Synagogue is dated 1407 though sources say 1407 or 1492 or 1570.
  • The Free City's ring is a rough pentagon around the city and is almost a point on the Poland-wide map.
