Exclusions

  • Before 561 BCE: Mycenaean Athens and its legendary kings (Theseus), the archons from about the seventh century, Draco (621) and Solon (594). A longer chapter would add an archaic house; the sources read give no sovereign list. Cylon's coup is not listed either.
  • Peisistratos's first two tyrannies are folded into one 561 to 527 reign, though he was twice expelled.
  • Democratic leaders left out: Aristides, Miltiades, Nicias, Phocion, Eubulus, Conon, Epaminondas, Thrasybulus's second career, and the generals of 411 to 403 other than Thrasybulus.
  • The Thirty Tyrants (404 to 403) and the oligarchy of 411: under about a year each, folded into the democracy.
  • The Macedonian and Hellenistic interludes: Lachares (296), Antigonus Doson, Demetrius II and the Ptolemaic naval presence; Athens' status between 229 and 88 as a free city under patrons is a judgement (see below).
  • Roman and Byzantine emperors not shown: all but eight of each house; no Julian, Constantine I, Trajan, Nerva or the Komnenoi. Herodes Atticus is shown as the patron of the Odeon, not as a ruler.
  • Slavic raids of 582 and later, the Arab raids and the Normans of 1147: not houses.
  • Boniface of Montferrat, who gave Athens to Otto de la Roche; Helena Angelina Komnene's regency; Joanna of Châtillon and the other Brienne titular claimants; the Aragonese vicars-general; Claire Acciaioli and Francesco I (1451 to 1454); Antonio II.
  • The Venetian rule of 1395 to 1402 (under eight years), the Venetian occupation of 1687 to 1688 (six months) and the Greek-held years 1822 to 1826 (four years): folded into the neighbouring houses.
  • The Bavarian regency of 1833 to 1835, the Second Hellenic Republic of 1924 to 1935 (eleven years), the Axis occupation of 1941 to 1944 and the colonels' regime of 1967 to 1974: no house of their own, since each ran under about 15 years or held power over a monarchy already in the data. The republic and the junta are described in the 1923 to 1973 captions.
  • Monuments left out: Hadrian's Arch and the Holy Apostles of the Agora (the page gave 1000-ish, "late tenth century"), the Pnyx, the Kerameikos, the Monument of Lysicrates, the Long Walls, the Olympic Stadium of 1896 as a separate building, the National Library, the University and the Fethiye and Fatih mosques, the Haseki wall.
  • After 1973: the Third Hellenic Republic (from 1974), the one state of Greece; the chapter ends where the monarchy ends.

Judgement calls

  • Athens' houses are regimes, not families: the Democracy is a regime whose "rulers" were elected generals, financial officers and speakers (and their years are years of influence, not terms), the Macedonian house mixes kings, a governor and later patrons, and the Kingdom of Greece is one regime spanning Wittelsbach and Glücksburg. Each house's character line says so.
  • The Macedonian house includes Antiochus IV and Attalus II as "patrons": Athens governed itself under them, but they paid for the Olympieion's restart and the Stoa, and the house would have no monuments without them. The confidence mark is "contested".
  • Democracy ends in 323 and the Macedonian house begins in 322: the pages give 338 (Chaeronea) or 322 (the Lamian War, Antipater's oligarchy); 322 follows the Antipater page. Hand-offs follow the one-year rule everywhere: the tyrants end 511 although Hippias fell in 510, Walter V's reign is shortened to 1310 though he died in 1311, the Ottoman house ends in 1832 although the garrison left in March 1833, and the Kingdom starts in 1833.
  • The Roman house runs from Sulla's 86 BCE to 395; the Byzantine house starts in 396 at Alaric's sack, a choice made so that the Roman house ends at the division of the empire.
  • The Catalan and Florentine regimes are one house; the Venetian podestàs (1395 to 1402) are folded in; Antonio I's reign is shown unbroken and Nerio II's too, though Antonio II ruled between 1439 and 1441.
  • The Ottoman start is 1458, the infobox and Ottoman Greece's date; the Duchy page's text says 1456.
  • Monument years are dates of completion or dedication where the page gave them, not starts: the Parthenon 432 BCE, the Hephaisteion 415, the Nike temple 420 and the Erechtheion 406. They sit under Alcibiades, whose years of influence cover them, although the work began under Pericles, as the Erechtheion page notes. The Propylaea is dated by its start, 437. The Theatre and the Stadium are placed under Lycurgus (330). The Olympieion stands under Hadrian although begun about 520 BCE by the Peisistratids. The Tower of the Winds (about 50 BCE) stands under Caesar although he did not build it: the Roman Agora, in which it sits, was begun under him. The Odeon, finished in 161, stands under Marcus Aurelius (he began in March 161) with Herodes Atticus as patron.
  • The kingdom ends in 1973 where the junta abolished the monarchy. Constantine II had been in exile since December 1967 and the king's real power was lost in the coup of April 1967, so a shorter chapter would end the kingdom there. The chapter ends at 1973 because that is when the monarchy ended. This is a choice the user may want to change.
  • Constantine I's and George II's reigns are each shown unbroken (1913 to 1917, then Alexander; 1922 to 1947 with the republic of 1924 to 1935 inside it, and Constantine's restoration of 1920 to 1922 not shown); the epithets say so.
  • The Roman house is linked to Rome in links.ts by the emperors' houses that appear here, and the Byzantine house to the Istanbul houses of the emperors named; the Ottoman house joins the existing Ottoman group.
