Exclusions

Before the range:

  • Nara, Nagaoka and the earlier settlement on the site (the Hata clan's Uzumasa and the Kamo shrines), down to 794.

Houses and rulers left out:

  • The imperial house as such. The emperors held the throne throughout, including under the shoguns, but as a house that held Kyoto they appear only in the Heian Court and Kenmu houses, because under the shoguns the real power lay elsewhere. After 1185 the court's emperors are not listed.
  • Heian: about 40 other emperors of the source list (Heizei, Junna, Ninmyō, Montoku, Kōkō, Suzaku, Reizei, En'yū, and others) and all the Fujiwara regents (Yoshifusa, Mototsune, Tadahira, Michinaga and the rest) who held much of the real power. This is an omission to keep the court arc short, not a historical gap.
  • Taira: the other members of the clan (Shigemori and others) and the child emperor Antoku.
  • Kamakura: the Hōjō regents (Tokimasa, Yoshitoki, Yasutoki, Tokiyori, Tokimune), who ruled in fact for most of the period; the source page did not list them. Also the Rokuhara officers who watched Kyoto, and the Jōkyū War of 1221.
  • Kenmu: Prince Morinaga, Nitta Yoshisada, and the Southern Court at Yoshino (1336–1392), which was not in Kyoto.
  • Ashikaga: Yoshimochi (1395–1428), Yoshikazu, Yoshikatsu, Yoshihisa, Yoshitane, Yoshizumi, Yoshihide and Yoshiaki (1568–1573, deposed; his reign falls inside the Oda house), and the lords of the Ōnin War, Hosokawa Katsumoto and Yamana Sōzen.
  • Oda and Toyotomi: Nobutada, Nobukatsu, Akechi Mitsuhide (who held Kyoto for about thirteen days in 1582) and Toyotomi Hidetsugu.
  • Tokugawa: Ietsuna, Ienobu, Ietsugu, Ieshige, Ieharu, Ienari and Ieyoshi, and the Shoshidai governors of Kyoto. The emperors of the Edo period (Go-Mizunoo to Kōmei) are not listed.
  • The Meiji emperor and the years after the capital moved (1868–1869 onward).

Monuments considered and not entered: Fushimi Inari Taisha and the Kamo shrines (older than the range; later rebuilding not dated here), Byōdō-in (in Uji, outside the plan), Heian Shrine (1895), Daitoku-ji (founding date uncertain), Sentō Palace, Shūgakuin, Jurakudai and Fushimi Castle (gone), the many Edo-period temples. No cut-off rule was applied beyond a few well-known buildings per house, so this list is only what came to mind and is not a survey.

Judgement calls

  • End year 1868: the source gives the Tokugawa shogunate as 1600–1868 and says the capital moved in 1868, with the court's transfer in 1869. The range ends with the Tokugawa house in 1868.
  • Houses are the powers that held actual control of the city, not the dynasties as such. The imperial house's continuity is noted above.
  • The Kamakura shogunate counts as a house that held Kyoto though its government was in Kamakura; Kyoto was watched from Rokuhara. Its start is 1184, when the Minamoto took the city, though the source's shogunate begins in 1192.
  • Hand-off years follow the rule that the next house starts the year after the last ends: Heian ends 1159; Taira 1160 to 1183; Kamakura 1184 to 1332; Kenmu 1333 to 1335; Ashikaga 1336 to 1567; Oda 1568 to 1582; Toyotomi 1583 to 1600; Tokugawa 1601 to 1868. Reigns keep their real dates within a year.
  • Takauji's reign starts in 1336 (when he took Kyoto), not 1338 (the shogun title). Hideyoshi's starts in 1582 (the year of Honnō-ji), a year before his house. Ieyasu's starts in 1601, though the source gives 1600 or 1603.
  • The Toyotomi house ends in 1600, though Hideyori held Osaka Castle until 1615, because Kyoto passed to the Tokugawa.
  • The rulers are named by their given names alone, as is usual in Japanese chapters; the house is already in the band above. The side effect is that the medallion initial repeats (every Yoshi- ruler shows Y).
  • "Descendant" is used for Heian emperors who are not children of the listed ruler, and for Tokugawa shoguns of branch families, so each lineage stays connected after skipped generations.
  • Kiyomizu-dera is placed under Iemitsu (the 1633 rebuilding), though it was founded in 778. Kitano Tenmangu is placed under Murakami (947), with the present buildings in 1607. Honnō-ji and Nishi Hongan-ji are dated 1591, when Hideyoshi moved them. Sanjūsangen-dō is dated 1164, though it burned in 1249 and was rebuilt by 1266. Kinkaku-ji is dated 1397, though the present building is from 1955. Kyoto Imperial Palace is dated 1855 (the present halls) under Iesada, with the shogunate as patron.
  • Realm rings show practical authority, not nominal claims: Ashikaga in 1467 and 1560 are drawn as only the Kinai and the Kyoto basin; Heian in 794 includes the Kantō though the frontier was still being pushed north. The 1868 ring includes Ezo (Hokkaido), which was only partly under Japanese control.
  • The realm map is framed on Japan (127 to 146° E), so the land outside it is shown at 50m detail from the widened world map, but no house here ruled it.
