London, United Kingdom

Built: 2026-10-31; extended to 1901 the same day. Range: 886 to 1901 (rail 850 to 1935). Opens at: 1675. Chapter 11. Data: src/data/london.ts, src/data/london-realms.ts.

Flag for the user: the end year is a choice. London's monarchy still exists, so the README rule has no literal answer. The first build ended in 1837 (William IV); that dropped Victorian London at the height of the empire, so the chapter now ends with Victoria's death on 22 January 1901, and the README rule is "constitutional monarchies end where executive sovereignty ended, or at the close of the empire". A shorter chapter would end in 1837, 1832 or 1688; a longer one would add Edward VII's Saxe-Coburg and Gotha (not built).

Exclusions

Before the range:

  • Roman Londinium, the Mercian kings of the 8th and 9th centuries (the History of London page says London came under direct Mercian rule in the 730s but gives no list of kings who held it, and the English monarchs list starts at Alfred), the Viking army of 871 to 886. Alfred's occupation in 886 is the first dated event the sources give for London itself.

Gaps shown on the rail: none; every house follows on from the last, and all hand-offs use the rule that the next house starts the year after the last ends.

Houses and rulers left out:

  • Æthelred of Mercia, governor of London for Alfred from 886 and until his death in 911. He was Alfred's son-in-law and held London in Alfred's name, and the chapter keeps London under Wessex from 886 (a judgement call, see below).
  • Ælfweard (16 days in 924), Æthelred the Unready's second reign (1014 to 1016) and Edmund Ironside (1016): see Judgement calls. Edgar Ætheling (15 October to 17 December 1066, disputed, never crowned) and the House of Godwin as a house: Harold II is a one-year house, folded into Wessex restored.
  • Matilda (7 April to 1 November 1141, never crowned, driven out of London by the citizens). Louis VIII of France (1216 to 1217, disputed claimant). Lady Jane Grey (10 to 19 July 1553). Philip of Spain (jure uxoris). Henry VI's second reign (1470 to 1471, six months) is folded into York. The Commonwealth years (1649 to 1660) have no monarch: Oliver and Richard Cromwell are listed in the Stuart house, which kept the house under 15 years of interruption.
  • Edward VII and the House of Saxe-Coburg and Gotha (1901 onward): the chapter ends with Victoria. The Hanoverian house is kept to 1901 although the Hanoverian electorate separated in 1837 (see Judgement calls).

Monuments considered and not entered: Lambeth Palace, Hampton Court (outside the plan), the Queen's House and the Royal Naval College at Greenwich, the Bank of England (the Soane building is demolished), St Martin-in-the-Fields, the Royal Exchange, Westminster Bridge and London Bridge (none stands), the British Museum, the National Gallery, Eltham Palace (too far east for the plan), Crosby Hall, the Charterhouse, All Hallows by the Tower, St Margaret's Westminster, the Crystal Palace and the Great Exhibition of 1851 (the Hyde Park site has no sourced coordinates and the Sydenham site, 51.4225 N, lies outside the plan; the building burned in 1936), the Albert Memorial, the Royal Courts of Justice, Westminster Cathedral, the Embankment and the Underground (1863). No cut-off beyond choosing eighteen well-documented survivors, so this list is only what came to mind and is not a survey. The York house, Edward the Confessor's house aside, has no monuments entered (Eltham, Edward IV's Great Hall, lies outside the plan); the Wessex and Danish houses have none (no building from their reigns is entered), so the monument tab says so.

Judgement calls

  • Start 886, not an earlier Mercian king. Alfred's occupation of London is the first dated event the sources give; earlier Mercian rule has no list in the pages read.
  • Under whose hand London lay 886 to 911. The History of London page says London was "placed under Mercian governance" with Æthelred ruling it, and that it passed to Wessex when he died in 911. The data puts Alfred's reign at 886 anyway, since Alfred's own page says he occupied London and put it in Æthelred's charge; Wessex held it as overlord.
  • Hand-off years. Wessex 886 to 1013, Denmark 1014 to 1042, Wessex restored 1043 to 1066, Normandy and Blois 1067 to 1154, Plantagenet 1155 to 1399, Lancaster 1400 to 1461, York 1462 to 1485, Tudor 1486 to 1603, Stuart 1604 to 1714, Hanover 1715 to 1901. Each house starts the year after the last ends, so a few reigns start a year before their house (Sweyn 1013, Edward the Confessor 1042, William I 1066, Henry II 1154, Henry IV 1399, Edward IV 1461, Henry VII 1485, James I 1603, George I 1714, Victoria 1837 to 1901 within Hanover).
  • The Danish house runs 1014 to 1042 although Sweyn took London in 1013 and died on 3 February 1014. The English returned under Æthelred (1014 to 1016) and Edmund Ironside (1016), whom London backed until Cnut won at Assandun; these two years are under 15 and folded into the Danish house, which is a distortion (London was under English kings then). Sweyn's reign is given as 1013 to 1014, though the house's era starts in 1014.
  • Wessex restored is a separate house from Wessex because the Danes stand between, and Harold II (6 January to 14 October 1066) is folded into it because the House of Godwin lasted under a year. Harold's relation to Edward is not a blood line, so he has no parent in the lineage.
  • Normandy and Blois: Stephen of Blois (1135 to 1154, 19 years, which is over the 15-year folding limit) is kept in the Norman house as a grandson of William I through his mother Adela, as the brief decided. Some lists treat Blois as its own house. Matilda is excluded.
  • Stuart folds the Commonwealth and Protectorate (1649 to 1660, eleven years). Oliver Cromwell and Richard Cromwell are listed as rulers; Oliver has no parent, Richard is his son. Charles II's reign starts in 1660, though he counted from 1649.
  • Lancaster 1400 to 1461: Henry VI's reign ends in 1461 (the list gives 4 March 1461 for the first end of his first reign); the York era starts in 1462. Edward IV's first and second reigns are one entry (1461 to 1483).
  • Monument placing. The Abbey is under Edward the Confessor (1065), though the building seen is Henry III's of 1245 to 1269, which the blurb says; Henry III has no pin of his own. Westminster Hall is under William II, with Richard II's roof in the blurb. The Temple Church is under Henry II, with the Templars as patron. St Paul's is under Charles II (foundation stone 1675), though the cathedral was completed under Anne, and the Royal Hospital Chelsea under Charles II (founded 1682, pensioners 1692). The Jewel Tower is under Edward III. The Guildhall is under Henry IV (begun 1411; finished 1440 under Henry VI). Kensington Palace is under William III with William and Mary as patrons. The Monument is typed "civic", since "tomb" would call it a mausoleum.
  • Origins. Wessex, Wessex restored, Lancaster, York, Tudor and Stuart are foreign: false with country "United Kingdom": the world map would otherwise highlight London's own country as foreign, and the Welsh and Scottish origins lie in the same country today. The Danes (Denmark), the Normans (France), the Plantagenets (France) and the Hanoverians (Germany) are foreign. Wessex restored gives Rouen, Edward's place of exile, as the route start.
  • Hanover in Germany is not drawn. It does not fit a single ring with the British Isles without covering the Netherlands, so the Hanoverian rings are the British Isles only, as the brief allowed; the origin story says so. Calais and the empire overseas are not drawn either.
  • Wales and Ireland are drawn whole from 1189 although Wales was held by overlordship until 1283 and Ireland in part (the Pale) until the Tudors; the captions say so where it matters.
  • Scotland is not in the ring until the Stuarts, since no earlier house held it.
  • Victoria stays in the House of Hanover. She was its last monarch; the lists name her house Saxe-Coburg and Gotha only from Edward VII, so no new house is needed before 1901. Her relation to William IV is entered as "nephew" (the type has no "niece"); she was his niece.
  • Hanover in 1837. The electorate went to Ernest Augustus, so the Hanoverian monarchs ceased to rule Hanover while the house name stays; the caption says so. The origin map still shows only the German homeland.
  • The empire frames are hulls. The Victorian empire is not one piece of land, so the 1858, 1886 and 1900 rings join the Isles to the Cape, Egypt and India by corridors along the sea road; they cover sea and a good deal of land the Crown did not hold (the whole of Africa in 1900 is inside the hull). The captions say so. Canada, Australia, New Zealand, the West Indies, Hong Kong and Malaya are not drawn. The RealmMap camera now shows a ring wider than 40 degrees whole (src/components/city/RealmMap.tsx, restCam), which no earlier realm reached except possibly the largest hulls of other cities.
  • Monument years. The Palace of Westminster is dated by the start of building (1840; work ran to 1870 or 1876), the others by opening (St Pancras 1868, Albert Hall 1871, Natural History Museum 1881, Tower Bridge 1894). All five sit under Victoria. Palace of Westminster is typed "palace", St Pancras and the museum "civic". The plan's top bound was raised from 51.525 to 51.535 to fit St Pancras.
  • William IV's reason for the former end date is from the source's wording ("the last British monarch to appoint a prime minister contrary to the will of Parliament"), which is narrower than the brief's "last monarch to dismiss a government"; the character line and epithet use the narrower wording.
