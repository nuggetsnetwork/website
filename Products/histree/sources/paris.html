Paris, France

Built: 2026-10-31. Range: 508 to 1870 (rail 480 to 1900). Opens at: 1700. Chapter 13. Data: src/data/paris.ts, src/data/paris-realms.ts.

Flag for the user: this chapter bends the "next house starts the year after" rule in several places, and it makes eleven houses. The decisions are under Judgement calls: the English occupation 1420 to 1436 is its own house (16 years, over the 15-year folding limit), which splits the Valois in two; Bonaparte is two houses; the Revolution (1792 to 1799) is folded into the First Empire with the Convention and Directory listed as "rulers"; the Robertian kings are folded into the Carolingians.

Exclusions

Before the range:

  • Gallo-Roman Lutetia, the Parisii and the Merovingian bishops before 508. Clovis's seat at Paris in 508 is the first dated fact the pages give for a ruler of Paris.

Houses and rulers left out:

  • The Merovingian kings who ruled elsewhere or outside Paris (Theuderic I, Theudebert, Sigebert, Childebert II, Theuderic II, Chlothar III, Childeric II, Clovis IV, Chilperic II, Theuderic IV); the mayors of the palace (Charles Martel, the Pepins). Charles Martel and the interregnum of 737 to 743 are within the Merovingian house's gap of 711 to 743 (kings listed 695 to 711 and 743 to 751).
  • Carolingians: Carloman I, Louis III, Carloman II, Charles the Fat (emperor and king 884 to 887), Louis the Pious's sons other than Charles the Bald, and the Robertian counts Hugh the Great and Hugh the Black as rulers (Hugh the Great held Paris and the dukedom, but is not on the king lists).
  • Capetians: John I (five days, 1316) and Philip V (1316 to 1322), to keep the court to 12; Charles IV is linked to Louis X as his brother.
  • Valois: no ruler was left out of the first Valois house; Charles VI's reign is cut at 1419 (see below).
  • Bonaparte: Napoleon II (4 to 6 April 1814 and 22 June to 7 July 1815, disputed), Napoleon's Hundred Days (20 March to 22 June 1815), General Cavaignac (June to December 1848), the Provisional Government of 1848, the Directors as individuals.
  • Louis XVII (claimant, died 1795), Louis XIX, Henry V ("count of Chambord") and Philippe VII: none reigned.
  • Haussmann, Richelieu, Mazarin and Colbert: ministers or prefects, not houses.
  • Everything after 4 September 1870, including the Commune and the Third Republic.

Monuments considered and not entered: the Tuileries Palace (burned in 1871, gone), Basilica of Saint-Denis (outside the plan), the Bastille (demolished 1789), Hôtel de Ville (burned in 1871, rebuilt), the Pont Royal and the Pont Alexandre III, Hôtel de Cluny, the Tour Saint-Jacques, Saint-Sulpice, the Sorbonne and Richelieu's chapel, the Gare du Nord and the Gare de l'Est, the Eiffel Tower (after the range), Versailles (outside the plan), the Sacré-Cœur (after the range), Haussmann's boulevards (drawn as a plan feature, not a monument), Napoleon's Louvre extension (Visconti and Lefuel, 1852 to 1857), the Bibliothèque nationale. No cut-off beyond choosing twenty well-documented survivors, so this list is only what came to mind and is not a survey. The first Valois house, Lancaster, the Carolingians, the Bonapartes' second house (one monument) and the Directory have none of their own, or few; the Valois and Lancaster monument tab says so.

Judgement calls

  • Start 508. Taken from the Conciergerie page, which says Clovis made the island his capital from 508. The Merovingian page says nothing of 508 and the Frankish list says only that Clovis "took his seat at Paris", about 509 to 511. A shorter chapter would start with Hugh Capet in 987 (the first house that made Paris the real capital) and drop the first two houses.
  • Hand-offs. Merovingian 508 to 751, Carolingian 752 to 987, Capetian 988 to 1328, Valois 1329 to 1419, Lancaster 1420 to 1436, Valois restored 1437 to 1589, Bourbon 1590 to 1792, First Empire 1793 to 1814, Bourbon Restoration 1815 to 1830, Orléans 1831 to 1848, Second Empire 1849 to 1870. Each house starts the year after the last ends, so a few reigns start a year before their house (Pepin 751, Hugh Capet 987, Philip VI 1328, Charles VII 1436 (the year he took Paris, one year before his house's 1437 start), Henry IV 1589, Convention 1792, Louis XVIII 1814, Louis-Philippe 1830, Napoleon III 1848).
  • The English occupation, 1420 to 1436 (16 years), is its own house. It is over the 15-year folding limit, and the lessons say a regime that held the city counts, so Lancaster (Henry V, Bedford and Henry VI) is a house of three rulers. The cost is that the Valois can no longer be one house: valois runs 1329 to 1419 and valois-restored 1437 to 1589, with Charles VII (reign set to 1436, when he took Paris, though he counted from 1422) the founder of the second. Charles VI's reign ends at 1419 in the data although he reigned to 1422 (Paris was in Burgundian hands from 1418 and the treaty of Troyes was in 1420). Henry V's entry into Paris in December 1420 and Bedford's death in 1435 are from memory or from the Henry VI page. Henry VI of England is a disputed claimant on the monarchs list ("French historiography does not count him"), kept here because he was crowned in Notre-Dame and Paris obeyed him.
  • Bonaparte is two houses, first-empire and second-empire, because ids must be unique and a house cannot have a gap inside it (the Restoration and the July Monarchy lie between). The names are the regimes, not "Bonaparte", since the First Empire house also holds the Convention and Directory.
  • The Revolution and First Republic, 1792 to 1799 (and the years to 1804, 12 years), are folded into the First Empire house rather than into the Bourbons (whose last king was deposed on 21 September 1792) or left as a gap. The house runs 1793 to 1814 and has three "rulers": the National Convention (1792 to 1795, a founder), the Directory (1795 to 1799) and Napoleon (1799 to 1814, linked to the Directory as a "usurper", for 18 Brumaire). They are regimes, not dynasts, so the lineage tree shows a break. The alternative, a 12-year gap on the rail, would have been shorter and plainer.
  • The Hundred Days (March to June 1815) are folded into the Restoration, not into the Empire: Louis XVIII's house runs 1815 to 1830 and he is listed from 1814. The Empire house ends at Napoleon's abdication in 1814, and reign for Napoleon ends then.
  • The Second Republic (1848 to 1852, four years) is folded into the Second Empire, which begins in 1849 with Louis-Napoleon as president (his reign is listed from 1848, when he was elected). Louis-Philippe's house ends in 1848.
  • The Robertian kings are folded into the Carolingians: Odo (888 to 898), Robert I (922 to 923) and Rudolph (923 to 936) hold the throne for 10 and 14 years between Carolingian reigns, under the 15-year limit, so they are three rulers of the Carolingian house with the relations the list gives ("usurper", "brother", "son-in-law"). They are not separate houses because a house cannot have a gap inside it. Hugh Capet, a Robertian count, starts the Capetians; the Capetian house is not entered as a continuation of the Robertians.
  • The Merovingian court is 12 kings of the 25 or so who reigned, chosen as those who held Paris or are famous; their reigns overlap in the sources (Chlothar II's reign starts 613 here, with Guntram to 592 before it; Theuderic III's and Childebert III's years are kingdom-wide, not Paris-specific). Reigns do not overlap in the data except Bedford and Henry VI.
  • The Bourbon house starts in 1590, but Paris was held by the Catholic League until 1594 (6 years, from memory), and Henry III had left Paris in 1588. Both are inside the house and the neighbour, so folded.
  • Monuments are under the ruler who began them, with later builders in the blurb: the Pont Neuf is under Henry IV (1607) though Henry III began it in 1578; the Place des Vosges is under Henry IV at 1605 although it opened in 1612 under Louis XIII; the Panthéon is under Louis XV (1758) though it was finished in 1790 under Louis XVI; the Arc de Triomphe is under Napoleon (1806) though inaugurated in 1836 under Louis-Philippe; the Madeleine is under Louis-Philippe (1842) though begun in 1763 and made a temple in 1806; the Chapelle expiatoire is under Louis XVIII (1816); the Palais Garnier is under Napoleon III at 1861, though it opened in 1875 after his fall; Notre-Dame is under Louis VII (1163) though the bishop built it; the Conciergerie is under Philip IV at 1300 (rebuilding ran 1285 to 1314); Saint-Eustache and the Lescot wing are both under Francis I. The Panthéon's monument id is pantheon-paris because Rome already has pantheon.
  • Types. "civic" is used for Les Invalides, the Place Vendôme, the Place des Vosges, the Arc de Triomphe and the Palais Garnier, since none fits the allowed set; the Louvre fortress is "fort", the Louvre wings and Palais-Royal "palace".
  • Origins. Merovingians (Tournai) and Carolingians (Herstal) are foreign, with country "Belgium"; Lancaster (England) and the Restoration Bourbons (Hartwell House, in exile) are foreign, "United Kingdom"; the Capetians (Paris), Valois (Crépy-en-Valois), Valois restored (Bourges), Bourbons (Pau), the First and Second Empire (Ajaccio) and Orléans (the Palais-Royal) are foreign: false, "France". The Restoration Bourbons' origin is their exile, not Béarn, which the Bourbon house already gives.
  • No overseas empire is drawn. Corsica is not in any ring (the Napoleonic origin marker sits on it), nor are Algeria, the Caribbean or Canada. Calais under England (1347 to 1558) and the Habsburg Netherlands are not separate shapes.
  • Region keys. gaul, ilefrance, normandy, anjou, aquitaine, gascony, provence and italy already existed and are reused. New: neustria, austrasia, burgundy, champagne, languedoc, germany. britain is not used.
  • Plan. fit: "ruler"; the bounds run 2.285 to 2.375 E and 48.832 to 48.88 N. The south arm of the Seine (Île de la Cité) is a second "river" feature; the Haussmann axis is the road.
