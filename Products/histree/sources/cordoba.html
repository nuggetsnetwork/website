Not verified

Written from memory, to check first:

  • Abd al-Rahman I's route into Spain (Damascus, Kairouan, Ceuta, Almuñécar), and the claim that he was a grandson of the caliph Hisham.
  • Almanzor's start of 981 (the source gives none), Hisham II's end of 1009 (he was restored briefly in the Fitna), and the Almanzor family links.
  • The Jahwarid reign dates of Abu l-Walid Muhammad (1043 to 1064) and Abd al-Malik (1064 to 1070) are only inferred from "about twenty-one years" in the source.
  • Al-Mu'tamid's reign in Córdoba (1070 to 1091), with Toledo's three years of control (1075 to 1078) folded in; the sources conflict.
  • That the Almohads took Córdoba in 1148, and everything about 1144 to 1147 (Ibn Hamdin's revolt, Alfonso VII's occupation); the Almohad end year of 1235, and how Ibn Hud's rule of 1228 to 1236 fits.
  • The Almohad relationships (Abu Ya'qub Yusuf a son of Abd al-Mu'min, and the next three each a son of the last).
  • The Córdoba monuments: the Synagogue's coordinates and Mudéjar style (the source gave 1315 only), the Plaza de la Corredera (1683), the Puerta del Puente (1571), the Triumph of San Rafael (1781) and its sculptor, the Calahorra Tower's rebuilding by Henry II in 1369 (the sources disagree; see below), and the coordinates of the Mihrab and the Cathedral Nave, which are guessed a few metres from the mosque.
  • Realm rings and region points. Every ring is drawn by eye; the roughest are the Maghreb rings of the Almoravids and Almohads, Almanzor's raids, and the taifas.
  • Origins: the places, stories and routes.
  • Plan features (the Guadalquivir, the road to Madinat al-Zahra): polylines drawn by eye.
  • The rail range (730 to 1960) and opening year (961).

Exclusions

Before the range:

  • Roman Corduba, the Visigothic period, and the Muslim governors from 711 to 756. The range starts with Abd al-Rahman I.

Gaps shown on the rail:

  • 1144 to 1147, between the Almoravids and the Almohads. It is real: Córdoba was held by local rulers and by Alfonso VII of Castile before the Almohads took it.

Houses and rulers left out:

  • Umayyad: al-Mundhir (886 to 888); Sulayman, Muhammad II and the other claimants of the Fitna, the Hammudid caliphs who held Córdoba intermittently from 1016 to 1026, and Abd al-Rahman IV and V; Sanchuelo, Almanzor's second son. About ten years of Hammudid rule are folded into the Umayyad house.
  • Jahwarid: Abd al-Rahman, the brother who lost the contest to Abd al-Malik.
  • Abbadid: Abbad I and al-Mu'tadid, who held Seville but not Córdoba.
  • Almoravid: Tashfin, Ibrahim and Ishaq, after 1143.
  • Almohad: Idris al-Ma'mun, Yahya and the later caliphs, and Ibn Hud, who held Córdoba from about 1228 to 1236.
  • Trastámara: Joanna, nominal queen from 1504 to 1555; Philip I, her husband (a few months in 1506).
  • Habsburg: the Archduke Charles, who claimed the crown from 1703 to 1715.
  • Bourbon: Louis I (1724), Joseph Bonaparte (1808 to 1813), Amadeo of Savoy (1870 to 1873), the presidents of the First Republic (1873 to 1874) and Serrano's regency (1868 to 1870). These years, about twelve in all, are folded into the Bourbon house; its calendar has no gap.
  • The Spanish Republic and Civil War, after 1931.

Monuments considered and not entered: the Roman Bridge (Roman, rebuilt in 719 before the range), the Caliphal Baths, the Almodóvar Gate, the Church of San Lorenzo and other fernandine churches, the Palacio de Viana, the Palacio de la Merced, the Gran Teatro, the Plaza de las Tendillas, the Archaeological Museum, the Alcázar's gardens (twentieth century). No cut-off rule was applied beyond a few well-known buildings per ruler, so this list is only what came to mind and is not a survey.

Judgement calls

  • End year 1931: Alfonso XIII left Spain on 14 April 1931. The README rule ends a range when sovereign dynastic power over the city ends, as for Kyoto (to 1868, though the Tokugawa ruled from Edo) and Rome (to 1946), so Córdoba runs past the caliphate (1031) and the Christian conquest (1236) to the monarchy. A shorter chapter, 756 to 1031 or to 1236, would drop the last four houses and everything after the Almohads; that was not asked for.
  • Hand-off years follow the rule that the next house starts the year after the last ends: Umayyad 756 to 1030; Jahwarid 1031 to 1069; Abbadid 1070 to 1090; Almoravid 1091 to 1143; Almohad 1148 to 1235; Castile 1236 to 1368; Trastámara 1369 to 1515; Habsburg 1516 to 1700; Bourbon 1701 to 1931. Reigns keep their real dates within a year.
  • Ferdinand III's reign starts in 1236 (when he took the city), not 1217. Philip V's starts in 1700, a year before his house. Peter I's runs to 1369, a year after the Castile house; Ferdinand II's to 1516, a year after Trastámara.
  • The Umayyad house covers both the emirate (756 to 929) and the caliphate (929 to 1031). Almanzor is listed as one of its rulers, though he was never a caliph, because he held the power for twenty years.
  • Isabella I is shown as a daughter of John II (the source gives half-sister of Henry IV). Charles III is shown as a brother of Ferdinand VI (a half-brother).
  • Eight of the nine Bourbon monarchs of 1700 to 1931 are listed; Louis I (1724) is left out.
  • Alfonso XIII's reign starts in 1886 (the year of his birth, as the source gives); Alfonso XII's in 1874.
  • Córdoba was not the capital after 1236; from then on it is a city held by the Crown, as Kyoto was held by the Tokugawa. The monuments reflect that: few after 1700.
  • The Great Mosque is dated to Abd al-Rahman I's foundation (786), though four later rulers enlarged it; the Mihrab and the Cathedral Nave are separate monuments for their own builders and sit a few metres from it.
  • The Calahorra Tower is placed under Henry II (1369), as a rebuilding. The Córdoba page gives an Almohad origin; the tower's own page says it was first built in 1333 by "Abu'l-Hasan the Almohad Caliphate", which cannot be right of the Almohads, who were gone by then, and does not mention Henry II.
  • The Torre de la Malmuerta is placed under Henry III (1406), who ordered it; it was finished in 1408 under John II.
  • Opening year 961: al-Hakam II's accession, with the caliphate at its height and the Great Mosque's biggest enlargement begun.
