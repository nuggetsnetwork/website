Exclusions

Before the range: Byzantium before 330 (Greek colony, then Roman provincial town), because the rule starts the range at the earliest house sourced for the city as a capital.

Houses and periods left out:

  • Phocas (602–610): a usurper, not a house. Shows as a gap between Justinian and Heraclian.
  • Twenty Years' Anarchy, 695–717 (Leontius, Tiberius III, Philippicus, Anastasius II, Theodosius III): no house. Shows as a gap. Justinian II's second reign (705–711) is inside the Heraclian house.
  • Nikephorian house (802–813) and Leo V (813–820): omitted for lack of monuments and to keep the list short. This is an omission, not a historical gap. The rail shows a gap 803–819.
  • Jovian (363–364): reigned about eight months in the handover year between Constantinian and Valentinian; not listed.
  • Valentinian I, Gratian and Procopius: western emperors or a usurper, not rulers of the city.
  • Tiberius II (578–582): left out of the Justinian house; Maurice is linked to Justin II instead.
  • Laskarid emperors at Nicaea (1205–1261): ruled from Nicaea, not Constantinople.
  • Leo II, Basiliscus, Zeno's break (475–476): folded into the Leonid house. Zeno is shown as one reign 474–491.

Rulers left out of houses that are included:

  • Macedonian: Alexander, Romanos I Lekapenos (his Myrelaion is credited to him as patron under Constantine VII, who was co-emperor), Romanos II, Nikephoros II Phokas, John I Tzimiskes, Constantine VIII, Zoë, Romanos III, Michael IV, Michael V, Constantine IX.
  • Komnenian and later: Alexios V Doukas, Isaac II's second reign (1203–1204), Peter of Courtenay, Yolanda, John of Brienne (regent emperor), John VI Kantakouzenos, Andronikos IV, John VII, Michael IX.
  • Ottoman: only 11 of 30 sultans are listed, because the court arc crowds past about 12. Left out: Selim II (dropped last, to relieve the arc; no monuments), Murad III, Mehmed III, Mustafa I, Osman II, Ibrahim, Mehmed IV, Süleyman II, Ahmed II, Mustafa II, Mahmud I, Osman III, Mustafa III, Abdulhamid I, Selim III, Mustafa IV, Abdulaziz, Murad V, Mehmed V. Their reigns are not on the rail as separate rulers.
  • Early houses: Arcadius, Constantius II and similar are included even without monuments, for the lineage tree.

Monuments left out (named, not entered): Church of the Holy Apostles and the Great Palace (no standing remains), the Serpent Column (older than the city), Boukoleon Palace ruins, Pammakaristos (Fethiye) Mosque, Beylerbeyi and Yıldız palaces, Anadolu Hisarı (on the Asian shore, outside the plan frame), Haydarpaşa Terminal. No cut-off rule was applied beyond keeping each house to a few well-known buildings, so this list is only what came to mind and is not a survey.

Judgement calls

  • Reign starts: Constantine I's reign is dated 330 (the city's dedication), not 306 or 324. Mehmed II's is 1453, not 1451.
  • Hand-off years: the next house starts the year after the last ends, so Palaiologan ends 1452 although Constantine XI died on 29 May 1453. Other shifted ends: Theodosian 456, Leonid 517, Amorian 866, Doukas 1080, Komnenian 1184, Angelid 1203, Latin 1260. Rulers' reigns keep their true last year, within one year of the house end.
  • The house named Doukas (1057–1080) bundles Isaac I Komnenos, Constantine X, Romanos IV, Michael VII and Nikephoros III, who were not all one family. It is named for the best known house in the group.
  • Golden Gate is placed under Theodosius I (built about 390) with the year 391, though it became the gate of Theodosius II's walls.
  • Hagia Irene is placed under Constantine V (the present church dates from after 740) although the first church was earlier.
  • Rumeli Hisarı is dated 1452, the year before the conquest, and sits under Mehmed II.
  • Yavuz Selim Mosque was not included because Süleyman I finished it; nothing is credited to Selim I.
  • The Ottoman house carries the plan opening year 1557 (the Süleymaniye), not the conquest.
  • Because sultans are skipped, the lineage tree links Ahmed I to Süleyman I and Ahmed III to Ahmed I with the relation "descendant" (added to types.ts for this), so the tree stays one connected line instead of three fragments.
  • Ottoman origin is Söğüt and marked "of the land", since today's Turkey contains it.
  • Realm frames: Hungary, Podolia and Yemen are included at their historical maxima; Crimea shows as held in 1481 to 1683 although it was a vassal khanate.
  • The Byzantine 1025 and Komnenian 1180 rings include sea between coasts, as do all rings.
  • Two photos use scans labelled "No restrictions" (Commons' label for Flickr Commons public domain). The fetcher's licence list was widened to allow that label.
