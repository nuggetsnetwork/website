Moscow, Russia

Built: 2026-10-31. Range: 1147 to 1917 (rail 1110 to 1950). Opens at: 1561. Chapter 12. Data: src/data/moscow.ts, src/data/moscow-realms.ts.

Flags for the user.

  • The Golden Horde is not a house. It held Moscow's lands by suzerainty from 1238 to 1480 (tribute, the khan's grant of the grand principality, the raids of 1238, 1382 and the like) but did not rule the city. The README rule says a house holds the city by actual sovereignty, and the princes who governed Moscow are the houses. The Horde is not drawn as a ring; the captions say what the princes owed (1238, 1252, 1264, 1380, 1463, 1485). A reader could argue the Horde was the true sovereign until 1480 and the chapter should show a "Golden Horde" band; I chose not to because it never administered Moscow and its seat was at Sarai.
  • The Time of Troubles is a short house (1599 to 1612, 14 years), against the lessons file's rule that interludes under about 15 years are folded. Neither neighbour could take it: the Godunovs, the pretender and the Polish prince were neither Rurikid nor Romanov, and folding them into either would credit the city to a family that did not hold it. If you prefer folding, merge Boris Godunov into the Rurikid house (to 1605) and start the Romanovs earlier, or leave a gap.
  • One family, three houses. The Rurikid line from Daniil (1263) to Feodor I (1598) is one family; it is split at 1462/1463 (Vasily II to Ivan III) because the princes stopped being tributaries. The Suzdal-Vladimir princes before Daniil are the same Rurikid line too (Alexander Nevsky is Daniil's father) and are a separate house because Moscow was then a border town of someone else's principality. A shorter chapter would start at 1263 and drop the 1147 to 1263 house (eight rulers, no monuments).
  • The chapter has 19 monuments (the brief said about 14 to 18); the Cathedral of Christ the Saviour is included with the caveat in its blurb (consecrated 1883, dynamited 1931, replica 1995 to 2000), and the photo is the replica.

Exclusions

  • The Golden Horde, as a house: see the flag above. Its khans are not rulers of Moscow.
  • Rulers dropped from the Suzdal-Vladimir house: Yaropolk III (1174 to 1175), Konstantin of Rostov (1216 to 1218), Svyatoslav III (1246 to 1249), Mikhail Khorobrit (1248), Yaroslav of Tver as regent and as grand prince (1264 to 1271), all for being brief or ruling Vladimir rather than Moscow. Mikhail Khorobrit's holding of Moscow in the 1240s is from memory only.
  • Rulers dropped from the Moscow house: Yuri of Zvenigorod (1434), Vasily Kosoy (1434 to 1435) and Dmitry Shemyaka (1446 to 1447), the claimants of the civil war, each months long.
  • Rulers dropped from the Tsars' house: Elena Glinskaya (regent 1533 to 1538).
  • Rulers dropped from the Troubles: Irina (disputed, January to March 1598), and the Seven Boyars as a body. The Polish occupation of 1610 to 1612 is folded into the Wladyslaw entry.
  • Rulers dropped from the Romanovs to keep 12: Feodor III, Ivan V, Sophia (regent), Catherine I, Ivan VI, Peter III and Paul I. Napoleon's occupation of 1812 is folded into Alexander I and noted in the 1812 caption.
  • Monuments left out: Sukharev Tower (1695, demolished 1934, not rebuilt), the Palace of Facets and the Kremlin's Armoury, Kitay-gorod's wall, Tsar Cannon, the Danilov Monastery (Daniil's founding is marked "citation needed" on the page, and the buildings are later), Dmitry Donskoy's white-stone Kremlin walls of 1366 to 1368 (gone), the Seven Sisters and the Sparrow Hills building (Soviet), the Moscow Metro. Peter the Great, Alexander II and Nicholas II have no monuments; the Yuryevichi, the Troubles have none that survive. Moscow's first stone churches under Ivan Kalita are lost.
  • Before 1147: nothing. Moscow is not named in sources before that, and the Slavic settlement of the site is not a house.
  • After 1917: the Provisional Government and the Bolsheviks. The chapter ends at Nicholas II's abdication on 15 March 1917 (his brother Michael declined the crown next day, which this chapter does not model).
  • Beyond the map: rings are cut at 148E (Kamchatka, the northern Okhotsk coast beyond 148E, Sakhalin, the Kurils) and Alaska (1799 to 1867) is not drawn. The map frame is [20..56E, 44..66N], so the Siberian part of every ring from 1584 is seen on the world map of The Ages only. The Siberian rings are a smooth outline of the claim, not of settlement.

Judgement calls

  • Hand-offs: Princes of Suzdal and Vladimir end 1263, Moscow's princes start 1264 (Daniil's reign is given from 1263, a year before his house), the Rurikid tsars start 1463 (Ivan III reigns from 1462), the Troubles run 1599 to 1612 (Boris reigns from 1598), the Romanovs start 1613. The checker allows a ruler one year outside the house.
  • Yuri Dolgorukiy's reign is given as 1147 to 1157, the years from the first mention of Moscow, not his 44 years as a prince of Suzdal.
  • Mongol overlordship: a house holds the city by actual sovereignty, so the princes of Suzdal and Vladimir, then of Moscow, are the houses; their captions say which years they were tributaries. One could say the khan was sovereign; I treated tribute and the khan's grant as suzerainty.
  • Same family, three houses (see flag). Ivan III is a "founder" root of the Tsars' house with no parent link, as his house starts a new status.
  • Relations: Yaroslav II is Yuri II's "brother" (a link to the previous ruler, not to the father, as London did); Ivan I is Yuri's "brother", Ivan II Simeon's "brother", Nicholas I Alexander I's "brother", Anna a "descendant" of Alexis (she was Ivan V's daughter, not listed), Catherine II a "usurper" under Elizabeth (she was Peter III's wife and took the throne from him), Peter II a "grandson" of Peter I, Alexander I a "grandson" of Catherine (Paul is skipped). Pretenders and Shuisky and Wladyslaw have no parent.
  • Peter the Great sits under the Romanovs for the whole 1682 to 1725 reign although he left Moscow in 1712; the capital was Saint Petersburg from 1712 (1721 to 1726 per one page) except 1727 to 1732. Moscow stays a Romanov city, and the coronation city, so the chapter keeps going. A shorter chapter would end in 1712.
  • Monument years: Spaso-Andronikov 1425 (the build runs 1420 to 1427); Kremlin walls 1495 (1485 to 1495); Archangel and the bell tower 1508 although begun under Ivan III's reign, filed under Vasily III because they were finished in his reign (Vasily III's patron is Ivan III in the blurb). Alexander III is given the Historical Museum (opened 1883), Christ the Saviour (consecrated 1883) and GUM (1893) though each was begun under earlier tsars. The Bolshoi is filed under Alexander I (opened 18 January 1825).
  • Moscow University's pin is on the Mokhovaya building, not on the Red Square medicine store where it began; the Stalin high-rise is not shown.
  • Mikhail Khorobrit, Elena Glinskaya and others dropped to keep the court arc readable (see Exclusions).
